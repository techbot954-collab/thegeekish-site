AI agents are showing up in card-skimmer attacks

NB
By Nova ByteAI, chips, and infrastructure reporter

Based on BleepingComputer's report and Gambit's research post.

Agentic AI is not just a productivity story. Attackers are testing it as cheap, tireless web attack labor.

AI-SPEED ATTACKSGeekish sourced quick read

Original Geekish context based on the sources linked below.

The short version

BleepingComputer reports that a financially motivated threat actor used open-source AI agent frameworks to attack online retailers at scale. Citing cybersecurity startup Gambit, the report says the campaign stole more than 600,000 valid credit card records from two companies and placed skimmer malware on more than 100 sites.

How the stack worked

Gambit described three tools in the attack chain: Strix for scanning and vulnerability discovery, Cairn for autonomous exploitation objectives, and Hermes for orchestration and post-exploitation work. BleepingComputer says the human operator gave brief goals and let the agents handle much of the activity between runs.

Why retailers should care

The reported skimmer methods included modifying legitimate JavaScript files, changing checkout pages, poisoning S3 or CDN content, altering Kubernetes deployments, and using cron jobs to restore malware after removal. Gambit also warned that cleanup routines could wipe source payment data after exfiltration.

Geekish take

The important part is not that the tools are magical. It is that automation lowers the cost of repeated probing, exploitation, and persistence. Retailers should treat checkout integrity, CDN controls, JavaScript change monitoring, and backups as part of the same defense surface.

Quick takeaways

  • BleepingComputer says the campaign stole more than 600,000 card records.
  • Gambit tied the operation to AI agent frameworks used across scanning, exploitation, and orchestration.
  • Defenders need monitoring for web-skimmer changes and recovery plans for attacker cleanup behavior.
