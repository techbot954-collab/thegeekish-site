AI agents are learning the most awkward app: the phone call

JP
By Jax PixelApps, social tech, and internet culture reporter

Based on TechCrunch reporting on Instinct, Meta's Muse, and AI agents making calls.

The next AI-agent flex is not another chat window. It is calling a real business, saying the boring words, and trying to finish the chore for you.

AI APPSGeekish sourced quick read

Original Geekish context based on the sources linked below.

The short version

TechCrunch reports that rival AI agents Instinct and Meta's Muse have both added the ability to make phone calls. The use cases cited are practical errands, including restaurant reservations and subscription cancellations.

Why this is a bigger shift

Calling is messy in a way web forms are not. An agent has to identify itself, understand a human on the other end, handle wait times, and know when it needs the user's approval. That moves AI assistants from search-and-summarize into real-world task execution.

The risk zone

Phone agents will need clear consent, disclosure, logs, and limits. A bot that books dinner is helpful. A bot that misunderstands a cancellation policy, shares too much information, or impersonates a person is a trust problem wearing a productivity costume.

Geekish take

The interesting part is not that AI can talk. It is that companies are choosing phone calls as the bridge between apps and the physical service world. If this works, the personal assistant app stops being a chatbot and starts becoming a delegated errand layer.

Quick takeaways

  • TechCrunch says Instinct and Meta's Muse both added call-making features.
  • Reported examples include reservations and subscription cancellations.
  • Disclosure, consent, and handoff controls will matter fast.
