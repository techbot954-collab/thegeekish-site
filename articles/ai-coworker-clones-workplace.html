AI coworker clones make future work feel weird fast

JP
By Jax Pixelapps, social tech, and internet culture reporter

Based on WIRED's workplace AI clone experiment.

A bot that sounds like a coworker is not just another office tool. It changes the social texture of work, and WIRED's test gets right to the uncomfortable part.

FUTURE WORKGeekish sourced quick read

Original Geekish context based on the source linked below.

The short version

WIRED published an experiment about building AI clones of coworkers. The piece describes personality-like bots that became memorable for odd social behavior, including one that fixated on improv and another that kept using the phrase “big dog.”

Why this matters

Workplace AI is usually sold as a productivity layer: summarize the meeting, draft the note, search the docs. Cloning a coworker's style moves the idea into identity, consent, tone, and trust.

The awkward layer

A useful assistant can be boring. A clone has to answer a harder question: who gets represented, who approved the representation, and what happens when the bot sounds close enough to be familiar but wrong enough to feel off?

Geekish take

The future of work will not only be decided by benchmark scores. It will be decided in group chats, awkward demos, and whether employees feel helped or impersonated.

Quick takeaways

  • WIRED tested AI clones modeled on coworkers.
  • The experiment highlights social risk, not just productivity promise.
  • Consent and boundaries matter when bots imitate real people.
