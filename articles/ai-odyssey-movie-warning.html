A full-length AI Odyssey movie shows why craft still matters

NB
By Nova ByteAI, chips, and infrastructure reporter

Based on The Verge's review of Odysseus: The Fall.

A 2.5-hour AI-generated movie is being pitched like a blockbuster. The review suggests the biggest lesson is how hard filmmaking still is.

AI FILMGeekish sourced quick read

Original Geekish context based on the source linked below.

The short version

The Verge reviewed Odysseus: The Fall, a 2.5-hour film from AI firm Fountain 0 that is billed as a fully AI-generated production. The verdict is rough: the review says the movie is full of distracting consistency errors, awkward voice work, and stitched-together scenes that do not add up to convincing cinema.

What went wrong

The review points to shifting character scale, mismatched mouth movement, odd physics, mispronounced names, and visual continuity problems. The larger issue is structure: short generated scenes are assembled into a feature, but the connective tissue that makes a movie feel intentional is missing.

Why creators should care

AI video tools are improving fast, but a feature film is not just image generation at scale. It needs blocking, editing, performance, pacing, sound, and hundreds of small human choices that keep viewers inside the story.

Geekish take

The useful takeaway is not that AI video is doomed. It is that "full-length" is a weak flex if the work cannot hold attention. The next serious leap in AI filmmaking will be less about runtime and more about control.

Quick takeaways

  • Odysseus: The Fall is described as a 2.5-hour fully AI-generated film.
  • The Verge says its glitches and consistency errors overwhelm the story.
  • AI video still needs better control before it can carry feature-length drama.
