Amazon Leo's next satellite internet stop is Uzbekistan

MC
By Mika Circuitgadgets, creator gear, and PCs reporter

Based on Engadget's report and Amazon's linked announcement.

Amazon's low-earth-orbit internet project is getting a practical coverage test: filling connectivity gaps across Uzbekistan's mountains, deserts, and remote communities.

SATELLITE WEBGeekish sourced quick read

Original Geekish context based on the sources linked below.

The short version

Engadget reports that Amazon Leo, Amazon's satellite internet service, is partnering with Uztelecom to bring connectivity to remote and hard-to-reach parts of Uzbekistan. Amazon's announcement says Leo is meant to complement Uztelecom's existing network, not replace it.

Why Uzbekistan is an interesting test

Uztelecom already reaches 96 percent of communities, according to Engadget's summary of the announcement. The remaining gaps are the hard parts: mountainous regions, deserts, and places where laying cable is difficult or expensive. That is exactly the kind of geography satellite broadband is supposed to help with.

What is still unknown

Leo has not publicly launched yet, and Engadget notes there is no live date for the Uzbekistan service. Amazon is still testing Leo with employees and select enterprise customers ahead of a wider rollout later this year.

Geekish take

This is the kind of partnership that matters more than a satellite count. Starlink is still far ahead in orbital scale, but Leo's real test is whether Amazon can turn launches and enterprise pilots into useful coverage for places that do not fit the easy broadband map.

Quick takeaways

  • Amazon Leo is partnering with Uztelecom for satellite connectivity in Uzbekistan.
  • The focus is remote and hard-to-reach regions where cable internet is harder to deploy.
  • No public launch date for the Uzbekistan service has been announced yet.
