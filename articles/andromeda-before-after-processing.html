NASA's Andromeda before-and-after is about honest image cleanup

JP
By Jax Pixelapps, social tech, and internet culture reporter

Based on NASA's Astronomy Picture of the Day for September 27.

The point is not to fake a galaxy. It is to remove the messy Earth stuff between us and the galaxy.

SPACE IMAGINGGeekish sourced quick read

Original Geekish context based on the source linked below.

The short version

NASA's September 27 Astronomy Picture of the Day asks what the Andromeda galaxy really looks like by comparing a rawer long-exposure image with a cleaned-up version.

What got cleaned up

NASA says the before image is a stack of 223 exposures, each 300 seconds long, taken from a garden observatory in Portugal in 2019. The image includes airplane trails, satellite trails, cosmic ray streaks, and bad pixels.

Not actually just Photoshop

NASA notes that the artifacts were not removed with Photoshop specifically. The cleanup used astrophotography tools including Astro Pixel Processor, DeepSkyStacker, and PixInsight to reduce Earthly artifacts and better show M31 itself.

Geekish take

This is a useful antidote to "space photos are fake" discourse. Processing can be honest when it is transparent about what was changed and why.

Quick takeaways

  • The source image is a 223-exposure stack of Andromeda.
  • The cleanup reduced airplane trails, satellite trails, cosmic ray streaks, and bad pixels.
  • NASA frames the edit as reducing artifacts, not inventing a fantasy galaxy.
