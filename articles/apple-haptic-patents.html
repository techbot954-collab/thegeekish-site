Apple's $5.7 billion haptics verdict turns tiny vibrations into a huge patent story

A San Diego federal jury sided with haptics company Taction in a patent case centered on vibration-based tactile feedback technology used in Apple devices.

MC
Updated September 28, 2026

Concise Geekish brief based on The Verge's Apple haptics report.

What happened

The Verge reports that haptics company Taction sued Apple in 2021 and has now been awarded more than $5.7 billion in damages by a federal jury in San Diego.

According to the report, the case centered on U.S. Patent Nos. 10,659,885 and 10,820,117, both involving vibration-based tactile transducer technology. The jury found infringement on two claims in one patent and one claim in the other, but did not find that Apple willfully infringed.

Why it matters

Haptics can feel like a tiny detail until it becomes part of the legal and product stack. The same feedback that makes taps, alerts, and watch interactions feel physical is also protected by patents, licensing fights, and jury verdicts.

Apple told The Verge it intends to appeal, so this is not the final word. But the verdict is a reminder that interface polish can carry very expensive intellectual-property baggage.

Quick takeaways

  • The damages award reported by The Verge is more than $5.7 billion.
  • The case involves vibration-based tactile transducer technology.
  • Apple says it plans to appeal the decision.
