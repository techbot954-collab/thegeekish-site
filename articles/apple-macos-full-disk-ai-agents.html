Apple is tightening Mac disk access as AI agents raise the stakes

Apple says it will add more controls around macOS Full Disk Access so broad access to files, mail, messages, and browsing history requires very explicit user action.

MC
Updated October 2, 2026

Concise Geekish brief based on Apple, TechCrunch, and The Verge reporting.

What happened

Apple posted a developer update saying Full Disk Access can sidestep normal app privacy controls and expose files, mail, messages, and browsing history. The company says some developers are using the permission in ways that could put users at risk.

The key line is about AI agents: Apple says that as agents become more capable and autonomous, the risks tied to this level of access will grow substantially. TechCrunch and The Verge both highlighted the change as a response to new AI-agent risk around Mac permissions.

Why it matters

Full Disk Access was built for powerful utilities such as backup apps, but agentic apps can act across files and apps in ways users may not fully expect. That turns a familiar Mac permission into a much bigger trust decision.

The practical signal: desktop AI tools are moving from chat windows into operating-system workflows, and platform owners are starting to draw sharper lines around what those tools can touch by default.

Quick takeaways

  • Apple says future controls will require very explicit user action for Full Disk Access.
  • The stated concern includes files, mail, messages, browsing history, and the privacy of people users communicate with.
  • AI-agent autonomy is now part of mainstream operating-system permission design.
