AstroForge wants an AI pilot for its next spacecraft

NB
By Nova ByteAI, chips, and infrastructure reporter

Based on TechCrunch reporting.

The asteroid-mining startup is testing a transformer-based autonomy stack called Solo, built for spacecraft that may not be easy to steer from Earth.

SPACE AIGeekish sourced quick read

Original Geekish context based on the source linked below.

The short version

TechCrunch reports AstroForge has built an in-house spacecraft autonomy stack called Solo. The system combines traditional controls, subsystem-specific models, and a transformer-based layer trained around roughly 2,500 spacecraft sensors.

Why the company wants it

AstroForge's earlier Odin mission had deep-space communication problems. CEO Matthew Gialich told TechCrunch the experience pushed the company to ask whether more intelligence should live onboard the vehicle instead of depending on scarce ground-network windows.

The flight plan

Solo is expected to fly in shadow mode on DeepSpace-2, which TechCrunch says is currently slated to launch with Intuitive Machines' third moon mission by the end of 2026. AstroForge then plans a 2027 Autonomy-1 mission on Stoke Space's first rocket, backed by NASA and expected to gather solar science data.

Geekish take

Space autonomy is not chatbot theater. If AstroForge can make a constrained model handle faults far from Earth, that is a useful preview of where AI belongs: narrow jobs, harsh conditions, and clear stakes.

Quick takeaways

  • Solo is a transformer-based autonomy stack built by AstroForge.
  • It is designed to help spacecraft respond to anomalies with less help from Earth.
  • The company plans a shadow-mode test before a 2027 autonomous mission.
