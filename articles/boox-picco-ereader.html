Boox is making the e-reader tiny enough to tag along

The Picco is a playing-card-sized e-reader with a 3.97-inch monochrome screen and a few productivity tricks hiding behind the tiny panel.

NB
Updated September 28, 2026

Concise Geekish brief based on WIRED's Picco announcement coverage.

What happened

WIRED reports that Boox announced the Picco, a palm-sized e-reader with preorders opening September 28. The device has a 3.97-inch monochrome screen, which WIRED describes as roughly playing-card sized.

The Picco is expected to cost $100 and ship in November. Its screen is listed at 235 pixels per inch with an adjustable front light that can shift between warm and cool tones. WIRED also notes Wi-Fi, Bluetooth, USB-C file transfer, and a microSD slot that supports up to 2 TB of storage, with a 16 GB card included.

Why it matters

The Picco sits in a fun little gadget category: smaller than a phone-sized e-reader like the Palma, but more capable than a novelty screen. Physical page-turn buttons, a magnetic ring for attaching to a smartphone, and lightweight apps like Pomodoro, Todo, and Countdown make it feel less like a pure book device and more like a tiny attention tool.

The catch is ecosystem convenience. WIRED points out that Boox's approach may still be less plug-and-read than Kindle or Kobo stores, but the tradeoff is broader file handling and a device small enough to become part of an everyday carry setup.

Quick takeaways

  • The Picco has a 3.97-inch monochrome screen at 235 ppi.
  • WIRED reports a $100 price and expected November shipping.
  • It includes reading controls plus Pomodoro, Todo, and Countdown tools.
