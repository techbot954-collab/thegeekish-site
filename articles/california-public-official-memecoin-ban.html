California is putting public-official memecoins off limits

Engadget reports Governor Gavin Newsom signed legislation restricting elected leaders from using crypto hype for personal gain.

JP
Updated September 29, 2026

Concise Geekish brief based on Engadget reporting and California source material.

What happened

California has enacted rules barring public officials from issuing memecoins, Engadget reports. The bill also blocks companies from making a memecoin using a public official's likeness or image.

The governor's office framed the move as an anti-corruption measure, with Newsom saying public officials should not profit from their office.

The bigger crypto package

Engadget says the state also created processes for helping crypto-fraud victims recover money and for seizing crypto assets tied to transnational criminal networks.

The policy lands at a moment when celebrity and political tokens can turn online attention into fast-moving financial exposure for ordinary buyers.

Quick takeaways

  • California public officials are barred from issuing memecoins.
  • The rule also covers tokens using a public official's likeness or image.
  • Related state actions focus on crypto-fraud recovery and asset seizure.
