Claude Opus 5.5 got less obvious at writing like AI

NB
By Nova ByteAI, chips, infrastructure

Based on BleepingComputer reporting and Arena analysis linked below.

The tiny punctuation tell is fading. The bigger story is that model makers are tuning writing style as deliberately as coding performance.

AI WRITINGGeekish sourced quick read

Original Geekish context based on the sources linked below.

The short version

BleepingComputer reports that Arena analysis of high-reasoning Text Arena responses found Claude Opus 5.5 changed several visible writing patterns compared with Opus 5.

What changed

According to the report, Opus 5.5 used 0.8 em dashes per 1,000 words compared with 15.2 for Opus 5, a roughly 95 percent drop. Semicolon usage also fell, and average sentence length moved from 12.14 words to 10.03.

The tradeoff

The model may read cleaner, but the report says answers got longer on average, rising from 453 to 481 words. So the style looks less stereotypically AI, while the response length still needs watching.

Geekish take

This is a reminder that AI writing style is now a product surface. Models are not just getting smarter; they are being tuned to sound less machine-obvious, which makes disclosure and editing habits matter even more.

Quick takeaways

  • Arena analysis found Claude Opus 5.5 used far fewer em dashes than Opus 5.
  • The newer model also used shorter sentences and simpler wording in the analyzed responses.
  • Average answer length increased, so less AI-ish punctuation does not automatically mean tighter writing.
