NHTSA is investigating comma.ai driver-assist devices

By Mika CircuitGadgets, creator gear, and transportation tech

Based on Ars Technica's September 23 report and the NHTSA investigation notice linked below.

Aftermarket driver-assist gear is getting a federal safety spotlight after crashes involving stopped or slow-moving vehicles.

Original Geekish context based on the sources linked below.

The short version

Ars Technica reports NHTSA's Office of Defects Investigation is probing aftermarket devices from comma.ai that can augment a vehicle's built-in advanced driver assistance systems.

What triggered the probe

According to Ars Technica, federal investigators know of five crashes in which drivers using comma.ai devices hit stopped or slow-moving vehicles. Two of those crashes resulted in three deaths, and 11 people were injured in four of the five crashes.

The bigger issue

Comma.ai grew out of the openpilot driver-assist world, where software and hardware can add more advanced automation behavior to consumer cars. The safety question is how much responsibility belongs to the device maker, the driver, and the underlying vehicle when that stack is layered together.

Geekish take

Driver-assist add-ons live in a tricky zone: powerful enough to change how people drive, but still dependent on humans and host vehicles. That gray zone is exactly where regulators tend to arrive late and loudly.

Quick takeaways

  • NHTSA is investigating aftermarket driver-assist devices from comma.ai.
  • Ars says investigators know of five crashes involving stopped or slow-moving vehicles.
  • Two crashes were fatal, with three deaths reported across them.
