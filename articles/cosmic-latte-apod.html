NASA's Cosmic Latte APOD makes the universe feel weirdly cozy

Today's Astronomy Picture of the Day revisits a delightfully geeky question: if you averaged the light of the universe into one perceived color, what would it look like?

MC
Updated September 28, 2026

Concise Geekish brief based on NASA's Astronomy Picture of the Day.

What happened

NASA's September 28 Astronomy Picture of the Day is "Cosmic Latte: The Average Color of the Universe." APOD explains that astronomers computationally averaged the light from a large sample of galaxies to produce a perceived composite color.

The answer is a pale beige shade represented as #FFF8E7. The name Cosmic Latte came from a naming contest, which is exactly the kind of astronomy-meets-internet detail Geekish lives for.

The nerdy part

APOD links the result to the 2dF Galaxy Redshift Survey and notes that the universe's composite color has become less blue over the past 10 billion years as redder stars have become more prevalent.

It is a small visual hook for a huge idea: even a question that sounds whimsical can sit on top of galaxy surveys, spectra, and the history of star formation.

Quick takeaways

  • Cosmic Latte is APOD's featured image for September 28, 2026.
  • The color is described as a conditionally perceived beige, with the computer value #FFF8E7.
  • The source image credits Karl Glazebrook and Ivan Baldry at JHU.
