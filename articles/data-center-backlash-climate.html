Data-center backlash is becoming a climate-tech story

Local fights over water, power bills, noise, and air pollution are now colliding with the bigger emissions question behind AI infrastructure.

JP
Updated September 28, 2026

Concise Geekish brief based on WIRED's Power Play column and linked energy analyses.

What happened

WIRED's Molly Taft writes that data centers were a flashpoint at Climate Week, where Extinction Rebellion NYC protesters interrupted a panel about AI and data-center infrastructure.

The column argues that public pushback has mostly centered on local concerns: water use, energy demand, power bills, noise, and air pollution. Climate impact is part of the movement, but it is not always the first argument communities make.

The bigger pressure

WIRED cites BloombergNEF analysis projecting that data centers could account for more than 5% of global electricity demand by 2035, with much of the new demand met by natural gas plants.

That makes data-center siting more than a local zoning fight. If AI demand keeps pulling new fossil-fuel generation onto the grid, the infrastructure story becomes a climate story whether operators frame it that way or not.

Quick takeaways

  • WIRED reports that data-center moratoriums and local opposition are emerging in both red and blue states.
  • Community concerns often focus on water, electric bills, air pollution, and noise before carbon emissions.
  • BloombergNEF projects data centers could more than triple their electricity demand by 2035.
