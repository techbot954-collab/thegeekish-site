Decap's drum packs are hiding in plain sight across pop music

JP
By Jax Pixelapps, social tech, and internet culture reporter

Based on The Verge reporting linked below.

The geeky tool story behind a lot of hit records is not always a synth, a DAW, or a plugin. Sometimes it is a drum sample pack with serious reach.

MUSIC TECHGeekish sourced quick read

Original Geekish context based on the source linked below.

The short version

The Verge profiles Nicholas Piantedosi, better known as Decap, and the Drums That Knock sample-pack series he launched in 2016. The Verge reports his sounds have appeared across work connected to major artists including Kendrick Lamar, Olivia Rodrigo, Charli XCX, Bad Bunny, BTS, Mac Miller, Freddie Gibbs, and Ludwig Goransson's Oppenheimer score.

Why producers care

Sample packs sit in the quiet plumbing of modern music. They can shape the snap, weight, and texture of songs without becoming part of the public-facing artist story.

What changed

Drums That Knock has reached its 11th edition, according to The Verge. That longevity matters because it turns a niche production tool into a recognizable creative layer for beatmakers, producers, and composers.

Geekish take

This is creator-tech at its best: a small, specific tool that ends up touching huge culture. The audience hears the impact before it ever knows the product name.

Quick takeaways

  • The Verge profiled Decap, creator of Drums That Knock.
  • The sample packs have been used across major pop, hip-hop, and film-score work.
  • The story is a reminder that music tech often becomes famous by staying invisible.
