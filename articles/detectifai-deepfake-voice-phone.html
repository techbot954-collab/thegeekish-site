DetectifAI wants deepfake voice checks to happen on the phone

After a family scam involving a cloned voice, the startup is building real-time fake-voice detection small enough to run directly on smartphones.

NB
Updated September 28, 2026

Concise Geekish brief based on TechCrunch reporting.

What happened

TechCrunch reports that Tarini Padmanabhuni founded DetectifAI after her grandfather was fooled by a deepfake of his brother's voice.

The San Francisco startup is building AI models small enough to run directly on smartphones and flag fake voices in real time. TechCrunch says DetectifAI is also competing in Startup Battlefield at TechCrunch Disrupt.

Why it matters

Voice scams work because they turn a familiar sound into an emergency. If detection has to happen in the cloud or after the call, it may arrive too late.

On-device detection is the geeky part here: smaller models can potentially respond faster, preserve more privacy, and work closer to the moment when someone is deciding whether a voice is real.

Quick takeaways

  • DetectifAI is focused on real-time deepfake voice detection.
  • TechCrunch says the company's models are designed to run directly on smartphones.
  • The founding story shows how AI safety can be deeply personal, not just abstract policy.
