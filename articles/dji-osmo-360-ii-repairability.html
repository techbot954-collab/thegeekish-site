DJI's Osmo 360 II makes repairability the action-cam headline

MC
By Mika Circuitgadgets, creator gear, and PCs reporter

Based on Engadget's September 23 review.

The spec bump matters, but Engadget's review makes one thing pop: a 360 camera built for rough shoots is a lot more convincing when damaged lenses are not a return-to-factory problem.

360 CAMERAGeekish sourced quick read

Original Geekish context based on the source linked below.

The short version

Engadget reviewed the DJI Osmo 360 II and rated it 8.7 out of 10, highlighting 8K 60 fps 360 video, improved low-light performance, better dynamic range, DJI microphone support, and replaceable lenses.

The repairability win

The original Osmo 360 required a much messier process if a lens got damaged. Engadget says the Osmo 360 II uses a new replaceable-lens kit that includes tools, instructions, cleaning gear, a suction cup, and gaskets. Reviewer Steve Dent says he changed one lens in under five minutes.

The catch

Engadget says the camera remains unavailable in the United States, even though it is priced at €589, or about $680. The review also notes that DJI Studio still needs work and that 360 footage can look softer than video from regular action cameras.

Geekish take

Replaceable lenses are exactly the kind of boring-sounding feature creators actually feel on location. If your camera is meant to be strapped to bikes, helmets, cars, and travel rigs, repairability is not a luxury spec. It is insurance for the next shoot.

Quick takeaways

  • Engadget rated the DJI Osmo 360 II 8.7/10.
  • The camera adds easier replaceable lenses and 8K 60 fps capture.
  • No U.S. availability remains the big downside.
