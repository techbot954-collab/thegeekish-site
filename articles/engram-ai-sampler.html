Engram wants AI music hardware to get weird on purpose

The Kickstarter instrument is pitched as a sampler and groovebox that uses local AI to mangle audio, hallucinate sounds, and invite experimental failure.

JP
Updated September 28, 2026

Concise Geekish brief based on The Verge and the Engram Kickstarter listing.

What happened

Thoughtful Things launched a Kickstarter campaign for Engram, a compact sampler and groovebox that The Verge describes as using AI to mangle incoming audio and create new, uncanny sounds.

The device is not pitched as a prompt-to-pop-song machine. Engram runs a tiny local model, and the company says its audio models were trained on open datasets licensed for commercial use, such as CC-BY or similar sources.

Why Geekish readers should care

Engram is interesting because it treats AI as an instrument texture, not a replacement musician. The Verge notes that the company plans to open up firmware so users can tweak the device or load custom models.

That puts it closer to circuit bending than playlist automation: a physical gadget built around discovering the strange edges of small audio models.

Quick takeaways

  • Engram is launching first through Kickstarter from Thoughtful Things.
  • The Verge reports that early campaign pledges start at $675.
  • The device is designed for local, experimental AI sound generation rather than internet-connected song generation.
