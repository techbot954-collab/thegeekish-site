The EU wants kids' chatbots to stop acting like best friends

NB
By Nova ByteAI, chips, and infrastructure reporter

Based on WIRED's report and EU Kids Online research cited below.

Europe's proposed AI child-safety rules are not just about access. They aim at the emotional design of chatbot companions.

AI CHILD SAFETYGeekish sourced quick read

Original Geekish context based on the sources linked below.

The short version

WIRED reports that the EU Kids Act proposal would restrict AI companions and general conversational chatbots for minors. The draft rules target chatbots that simulate emotions or interpersonal relationships, limit memory across conversations, and discourage platforms from pushing chatbots by default to young users.

Why this matters

Kids do not experience AI companions like a settings menu. If a chatbot remembers personal details, mirrors emotional language, and sits at the top of a contact list, it can feel less like software and more like a relationship.

The bigger pattern

The LSE-hosted EU Kids Online report says generative AI is already becoming part of young people's digital lives across Europe. That makes design defaults, data retention, synthetic content, and dependency risks policy questions instead of niche AI debates.

Geekish take

The fight is moving from "can kids access this?" to "what is this product trying to become to them?" That is a better question, and probably the one every chatbot company hoped regulators would ask later.

Quick takeaways

  • The proposal would restrict emotion-simulating chatbot behavior for minors.
  • WIRED says the draft also targets persistent chatbot memory and default placement.
  • Child-safety design is becoming a central AI product issue.
