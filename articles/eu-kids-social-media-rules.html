The EU wants stricter social rules for kids under 16

JP
By Jax Pixelapps, social tech, and internet culture reporter

Based on WIRED's reporting on the European Commission's planned EU Kids Act.

Europe is moving toward a tiered social media plan: no personal accounts for under-13s, supervised mini accounts for 13-to-15-year-olds, and safer design rules for users up to 18.

SOCIAL POLICYGeekish sourced quick read

Original Geekish context based on the source linked below.

The short version

WIRED reports that European Commission President Ursula von der Leyen announced plans to restrict children's access to social media across EU member states as part of an upcoming EU Kids Act.

What is proposed

According to WIRED, the proposal would ban social media for children under 13. Children aged 13 to 15 would not get personal accounts, but could use parent- or guardian-supervised mini accounts with limited features for one hour per day.

The hard part

The plan still has to move through the EU process, and enforcement is the messy bit. WIRED notes concerns around age checks, privacy, parental monitoring, and whether young users will simply work around the rules.

Geekish take

This is the next phase of the child safety fight: not just "should kids be online," but who verifies age, who supervises accounts, and how platforms redesign feeds without turning privacy into collateral damage.

Quick takeaways

  • The EU plan would restrict social media access for children under 16.
  • Under-13s would be blocked from social media under the reported proposal.
  • Age checks, privacy, and enforcement are likely to be the main fights.
