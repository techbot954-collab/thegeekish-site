F-Droid 2.0 gives the open-source Android store its biggest update in years

JP
By Jax PixelApps, social tech, and internet culture reporter

Based on F-Droid's announcement and Ars Technica reporting linked below.

The alternative Android app store is trying to feel less like a time capsule and more like a modern place to find free and open-source apps.

ANDROIDGeekish sourced quick read

Original Geekish context based on the sources linked below.

The short version

F-Droid says version 2.0 is its largest official app update in 10 years. The new client is rolling out after 14 test releases and brings a redesigned interface built with Kotlin Compose.

What changed

The new app reorganizes the experience around Discover, Search, and My Apps. F-Droid says discovery now highlights new, updated, and most-downloaded apps, while search can look across names, descriptions, categories, and translated content.

The Android-store angle

Ars Technica notes the update also makes installs smoother on supported devices through newer Android APIs, bringing F-Droid closer to the experience people expect from built-in app stores.

Geekish take

F-Droid has always had the right mission for privacy-minded Android users. The hard part was making a huge open-source catalog feel browsable. Version 2.0 sounds like the project finally treating usability as part of the freedom story.

Quick takeaways

  • F-Droid 2.0 is described by the project as its largest app update in 10 years.
  • The redesign adds clearer Discover, Search, and My Apps areas.
  • Search, categories, filtering, and installs have all been reworked.
