A cut fiber cable turned into a flight-delay mess

MC
By Mika CircuitGadgets, creator gear, and PCs reporter

Based on The Verge's September 21 report.

Hundreds of delayed or canceled flights came down to a very old internet lesson: buried cables still run the world.

PHYSICAL CLOUDGeekish sourced quick read

Original Geekish context based on the source linked below.

The short version

The Verge reports that construction crews in New Jersey accidentally cut a Verizon fiber cable used for air traffic control, causing hundreds of flight cancellations or delays across the US.

The disruption spread fast

The report says the outage hit airports across the Northeast and triggered ground stops at all three major New York City airports. By Monday evening, Transportation Secretary Sean Duffy said the telecom lines had been repaired and operations were resuming.

Why it matters

Air traffic control sounds like a software story until a cable in the ground gets cut. This is the boring-but-critical layer of modern tech: physical routes, contractors, backup paths, and the handoff between telecom and public infrastructure.

Geekish take

The cloud has a shovel problem. One damaged line should not feel like a national travel event, so the real follow-up is redundancy: what has to be duplicated, rerouted, or hardened before the next accidental cut?

Quick takeaways

  • The Verge says a cut Verizon fiber cable disrupted flights across the US.
  • The outage affected Northeast airports and caused New York City ground stops.
  • Officials said repairs were made, but delays would take time to clear.
