FAA laser-strike reports are down, but 4,470 is still a lot

JP
By Jax Pixelapps, social tech, and internet culture reporter

Based on an FAA safety bulletin and Engadget's report.

Reported aircraft laser strikes fell for a third year, but the FAA's numbers are still a reminder that a cheap gadget can become a cockpit hazard fast.

AIRSPACE SAFETYGeekish sourced quick read

Original Geekish context based on the sources linked below.

The short version

The FAA says pilots reported 4,470 laser strikes in 2026 through July, a 24 percent drop from the same point in 2025 and the third straight year of decline.

The bigger number

The agency says laser strikes hit an all-time high of 13,304 in 2023, and pilots have reported 322 injuries since the FAA began tracking these reports in 2010.

The penalty reminder

The FAA says shining a laser at an aircraft is a federal crime. Civil fines can reach $11,000 per violation, and criminal penalties can include prison time and a $250,000 fine.

Geekish take

This is one of those low-tech problems that feels weirdly modern: consumer lasers are everywhere, but a beam pointed skyward can become a safety incident before the person holding it understands what happened.

Quick takeaways

  • FAA laser-strike reports are down for a third consecutive year.
  • Pilots still reported 4,470 strikes from January through July 2026.
  • The FAA is warning people not to aim holiday laser displays into the sky.
