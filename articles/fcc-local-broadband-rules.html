Cities are pushing back on the FCC's broadband preemption plan

NB
By Nova ByteAI, chips, and infrastructure reporter

Based on Ars Technica reporting and the FCC proposal.

The FCC says local permitting slows broadband deployment. Cities say the plan would override safety rules and ignore how providers can block competition.

BROADBAND POLICYGeekish sourced quick read

Original Geekish context based on the sources linked below.

The short version

Ars Technica reports that cities and counties across the US are objecting to an FCC proposal that would preempt state and local broadband permitting rules. The FCC argues some local processes delay builds and make deployment more expensive.

Why cities object

Local governments told the FCC that permit requirements help protect public safety and manage streets, poles, and rights-of-way. Ars also reports that some local comments argue the FCC should focus on provider behavior, including permit-hoarding and tactics that slow rivals.

Geekish take

Broadband policy is infrastructure in plain clothes. Faster deployment matters, but so does who controls the streets and whether a shortcut for incumbents actually creates better internet for neighborhoods.

Quick takeaways

  • The FCC proposal targets local broadband permitting rules.
  • Cities say the plan could override safety and right-of-way protections.
  • Broadband providers support streamlining, according to Ars.
