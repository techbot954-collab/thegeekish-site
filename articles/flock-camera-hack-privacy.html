A hacked Flock camera shows why license-plate readers are bigger than plates

JP
By Jax Pixelapps, social tech, and internet culture reporter

Based on WIRED and 404 Media's joint reporting and prior Flock security disclosures linked below.

A removed Flock camera gave reporters a look at what the roadside system stores and detects. The story is not only license plates. It is how much context a camera can collect while doing that job.

PRIVACY TECHGeekish sourced quick read

Original Geekish context based on the sources linked below.

The short version

WIRED reports that hackers removed a Flock camera, copied much of the data inside it, and shared material with 404 Media and WIRED. The analysis showed how the system captures and processes roadway images.

What reporters found

According to WIRED, recovered logs covered about 21 days across several periods. The camera photographed roughly 50,200 vehicles and generated about 1.6 million images. The analysis also found that the camera software explicitly detects people, along with vehicles, plates, and bicycles.

What Flock says

Flock has said its cameras do not perform face recognition. WIRED and 404 Media reported that they found no evidence of face-recognition capabilities in the camera's software beyond default Android operating system components.

Geekish take

Automatic license-plate readers are often framed as narrow tools. This report makes the broader question harder to dodge: when a camera watches a street all day, how much non-plate information becomes part of the machine?

Quick takeaways

  • WIRED and 404 Media analyzed data from a removed Flock camera.
  • The recovered logs included about 50,200 vehicles and 1.6 million images.
  • The reporting says the software detects people as well as vehicles and plates.
