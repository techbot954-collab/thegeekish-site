Flock camera sourcing questions turn surveillance hardware into a supply-chain story

NB
By Nova ByteAI, chips, and infrastructure reporter

Based on WIRED's September 17 report and Flock Safety's public company materials.

License plate readers are usually debated as privacy tech. WIRED's latest Flock story adds another layer: where the hardware is assembled and how much public buyers get to know.

PRIVACY TECHGeekish sourced quick read

Original Geekish context based on the sources linked below.

The short version

WIRED reports that Flock Safety reveals little about where its license plate reader cameras are assembled, even though the company previously touted its cameras as "Made in the USA."

Why that matters

Flock cameras are used by communities, businesses, and law enforcement to capture vehicle data. WIRED frames the sourcing question as more than a label dispute because surveillance hardware also carries cybersecurity, procurement, and geopolitical risk.

The broader pattern

The report lands as connected camera networks keep moving from local security tools into shared data infrastructure. That makes hardware transparency more important: agencies and neighborhoods are not just buying a camera, they are buying a supply chain attached to sensitive public-space data.

Geekish take

The privacy debate around automated license plate readers usually focuses on who can search the data. This update is a reminder to ask a second question: who builds the devices collecting it? For public tech, trust has to include the box on the pole, not just the dashboard in the cloud.

Quick takeaways

  • WIRED says Flock reveals little about where its ALPR cameras are assembled.
  • The question touches privacy, cybersecurity, and government procurement.
  • Surveillance hardware transparency is becoming part of the trust story.
