Flock's camera network got a rough day in Washington

JP
By Jax Pixelapps, social tech, and internet culture reporter

Based on The Verge reporting linked below.

Automated license plate readers have moved from local city council fights to a Senate hearing, and the questions are getting sharper.

SURVEILLANCE TECHGeekish sourced quick read

Original Geekish context based on the source linked below.

The short version

The Verge reports Flock was scrutinized at a Senate Judiciary subcommittee hearing about its "AI Surveillance Network," even though Flock's CEO declined to testify.

What lawmakers focused on

According to The Verge, senators raised concerns about automated license plate readers collecting location data, how long that information can be stored, who can search it, and whether outside agencies could route requests through local partners.

The wider signal

The fight around camera networks is no longer just about one company. It is about whether cheap, searchable location infrastructure can quietly become a national tracking layer before the rules catch up.

Geekish take

Useful public-safety tech still needs limits people can understand. If camera networks are powerful enough to build dossiers, the audit logs, warrants, retention rules, and abuse penalties need to be just as real as the hardware.

Quick takeaways

  • The Verge says Flock's CEO declined to testify at the Senate hearing.
  • Lawmakers questioned data access, retention, security, and cross-agency searches.
  • The policy fight around automated license plate readers is escalating.
