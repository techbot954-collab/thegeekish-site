Big Tech's anti-terrorism group has an oversight problem

Six independent experts reportedly resigned from GIFCT's advisory board after proposed changes they believed would blunt scrutiny of the tech-company-led consortium.

NB
Updated September 28, 2026

Concise Geekish brief based on WIRED reporting and GIFCT governance material.

What happened

WIRED reports that six of nine independent experts on the Global Internet Forum to Counter Terrorism's advisory board resigned Monday. The departing advisers objected to proposed changes that they said would reduce the group's ability to scrutinize the consortium's work.

GIFCT's own governance page says the organization is governed by an operating board from founding companies Meta, Microsoft, and YouTube, with Discord and Twitch elected as at-large members for 2026. It also says Meta is the 2026 chair.

Why it matters

Content moderation is already a trust problem. When the same companies that operate platforms also control the oversight structure around harmful-content coordination, independent review becomes more than process theater.

The timing is rough. WIRED notes that extremist material, AI-generated propaganda, and online abuse networks remain persistent threats. Weakening watchdog input may make the consortium easier to manage internally, but harder to trust externally.

Quick takeaways

  • WIRED says six independent advisers resigned from GIFCT's advisory board.
  • The advisers objected to changes they believed would limit scrutiny and collective recommendations.
  • GIFCT says its Independent Advisory Committee includes civil society, government, and intergovernmental representatives.
