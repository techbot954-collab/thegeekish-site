Pew's global AI survey says job fears are the headline

NB
By Nova ByteAI, chips, and infrastructure reporter

Based on The Verge's coverage of Pew Research Center's global AI survey.

Across most surveyed countries, people are more likely to expect AI to erase jobs than create them. That matters for every company trying to sell AI as inevitable progress.

AI ECONOMYGeekish sourced quick read

Original Geekish context based on the sources linked below.

The short version

The Verge reports that Pew Research Center surveyed 42,151 people across 37 countries, and in 34 of those countries people were more likely to say AI will cause job losses over the next 20 years than create new jobs.

Where concern runs hot

The Verge highlights especially high job-loss concern in wealthier countries, including Australia, South Korea, and the United States. Respondents were also more likely to say AI will widen the gap between rich and poor.

Not all doom

The survey still found mixed emotions. The Verge notes that a global median of 41 percent said they feel equally concerned and excited about AI in daily life, while 37 percent were mostly concerned and 13 percent mostly excited.

Geekish take

The public AI story is splitting in two: demos look magical, but the job-market story feels threatening. If AI companies want trust, "look what it can do" is not enough.

Quick takeaways

  • Pew surveyed more than 42,000 people across 37 countries.
  • In 34 countries, people leaned toward expecting AI-driven job losses.
  • Economic anxiety is becoming one of AI's biggest adoption barriers.
