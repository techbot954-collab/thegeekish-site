Google ads are still a scareware delivery lane

NB
By Nova ByteAI, chips, and infrastructure reporter

Based on Ars Technica reporting linked below.

The scam is old, but the delivery path is very modern: a web ad, a frozen screen, and a fake support number.

SECURITYGeekish sourced quick read

Original Geekish context based on the source linked below.

The short version

Ars Technica reports researchers found Google ads delivering convincing tech-support scareware. The ads could freeze Windows and Mac screens and push victims toward bogus call centers.

How the trap works

The pitch is familiar: your computer is supposedly infected, the screen looks urgent, and a phone number appears to offer help. Ars says users who called could be pressured to pay fees, share personal information, or grant remote access.

Why it matters

People tend to treat ads on known sites as safer than random popups. That trust gap is exactly why malvertising keeps coming back: a legitimate ad pipeline can still become the first step in a scam.

Geekish take

The practical rule is simple: do not call the number on a panic screen. Close the browser, restart if needed, and use security tools or support contacts you chose yourself.

Quick takeaways

  • Ars reports Google ads were used to deliver tech-support scareware.
  • The campaign targeted both Windows and Mac users with frozen-screen warnings.
  • The safest response is to avoid any phone number shown by the warning page.
