Google is backing civic AI tools for local governments

JP
By Jax PixelApps, social tech, and internet culture reporter

Based on Google's announcement and Jigsaw program materials.

Google.org is supporting Jigsaw's Sensemaking AI partner program, which offers open-source tools for large-scale civic conversations.

CIVIC AIGeekish sourced quick read

Original Geekish context based on the sources linked below.

The short version

Google says Google.org is supporting Jigsaw's new partner program and a collaborative fund to help local governments use Sensemaking AI, a suite of open-source tools for public conversations and community feedback.

What the tools do

According to Google's announcement, localities can use the program to run fast, large-scale civic conversations at no operational cost, then surface themes that help leaders understand community needs.

Where it has shown up

Google says Bowling Green, Kentucky, used input from 8,000 residents to inform a 25-year growth plan. The announcement also names Chattanooga, Riverside, Kitchener, and other cities as places with similar efforts underway.

Geekish take

The good version of civic AI is not replacing public input. It is making messy public input readable enough that more voices can count. The hard part, as always, is making sure the summary does not become the decision.

Quick takeaways

  • Google.org is supporting Jigsaw's Sensemaking AI partner program.
  • The tools are meant to help governments collect and interpret large-scale public feedback.
  • Google says Bowling Green used feedback from 8,000 residents for long-term planning.
