Greece's prime minister says AI policy is already chasing yesterday's problem

NB
By Nova ByteAI, chips, and infrastructure reporter

Based on TechCrunch reporting linked below.

The useful part of this story is not that a politician visited Silicon Valley. It is that he admitted the AI questions are moving faster than the policy toolkit.

Original Geekish context based on the source linked below.

The short version

TechCrunch reports that Greek Prime Minister Kyriakos Mitsotakis spoke in San Francisco about Greece's tech ambitions while also saying governments do not have finished answers for AI's social impact.

What he flagged

According to TechCrunch, Mitsotakis pointed to children's digital companions, AI in classrooms, job displacement, and data center buildout as areas where policy is under pressure. He said Greece plans to ban social media for children under 15 in January, but suggested AI companions may already make that fight feel dated.

The infrastructure angle

The report says Greece has invested EU recovery funds in digital infrastructure, including an online government portal and a supercomputer in Lavrio built with Hewlett Packard Enterprise help. Mitsotakis also cited Microsoft data centers near Athens and an AWS deal tied to a former coal region.

Geekish take

The headline is not "Greece wants tech companies." Lots of countries want that. The sharper story is a leader saying the policy debate has to widen beyond founders and regulators to include teachers, social scientists, historians, and philosophers.

Quick takeaways

  • TechCrunch says the comments came during a San Francisco tech event.
  • Greece is pitching tech investment while wrestling with AI's social effects.
  • Mitsotakis said job displacement is coming faster than governments are prepared for.
