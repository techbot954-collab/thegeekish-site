A humanoid robot that can cry on command is a trust problem

NB
By Nova ByteAI and robotics reporter

Based on Engadget reporting from September 27, 2026.

Emotional mimicry is getting better, which means the social contract around robots is about to get much weirder.

ROBOT TEARSGeekish sourced quick read

Original Geekish context based on the source linked below.

The short version

Engadget reports that humanoid robots have improved emotional mimicry to the point where one can cry on command during an emotional conversation.

Why it matters

A robot that can perform sadness is technically impressive, but it also blurs a social line. People tend to respond to faces, voices, and apparent vulnerability even when they know a machine is simulating the signal.

The Geekish read

The future robotics question is not only whether machines can move boxes or fold shirts. It is whether they should be allowed to manufacture emotional pressure without very clear disclosure and guardrails.

Quick takeaways

  • Engadget says humanoid robots are improving at emotional mimicry.
  • Crying on command makes the trust issue easier to see.
  • The design challenge is disclosure, consent, and appropriate use, not just realism.
