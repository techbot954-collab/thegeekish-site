The iPhone Duo's virtual Walkman idea gets the foldable joke

A cassette-style music app shows why foldables need more than bigger screens: they need interactions that make the hinge feel like the point.

JP
Updated September 28, 2026

Concise Geekish brief based on TechCrunch reporting about the virtual Walkman app concept.

What happened

TechCrunch reports that the rumored iPhone Duo may already have an early standout app: a virtual Walkman. The app imitates an old cassette player by letting someone open the foldable, pick music, insert a cassette-style interface, and then close the device to listen.

It is a tiny idea with a very geeky lesson. A foldable phone is not automatically exciting because it bends. It gets interesting when software treats the fold as a control surface.

Why it matters

Most foldable demos drift toward productivity: bigger canvases, split apps, drag-and-drop work. The virtual Walkman angle is different. It uses nostalgia and physical ritual to make a new device shape feel emotionally obvious.

That is exactly how odd hardware earns a place in people's routines. The killer app may not be a spreadsheet. Sometimes it is the thing that makes you grin the first time you close the screen.

Quick takeaways

  • TechCrunch says the app mimics choosing and inserting a cassette.
  • The interaction depends on opening and closing the foldable device.
  • It is a reminder that new hardware needs native-feeling software rituals.
