Kairos, Samsung, and Google show how AI power deals are getting real

MC
By Mika Circuitgadgets, commerce, and creator gear reporter

Based on TechCrunch's September 21 report.

The AI infrastructure story keeps expanding beyond chips and data centers. Now the supporting cast includes nuclear developers, construction partners, and very specific megawatt targets.

CLEAN POWERGeekish sourced quick read

Original Geekish context based on the source linked below.

The short version

TechCrunch reports that Kairos Power has a deal for up to $100 million from Samsung C&T to help build its first 50-megawatt nuclear power plant. Kairos is already connected to Google's plans for future data-center power.

Why it matters

AI power demand has turned clean electricity from a sustainability talking point into a strategic supply-chain issue. Data center operators want firm power that can run around the clock, and advanced nuclear is one of the options being pushed into that conversation.

The Google connection

Google signed a deal with Kairos in 2024 to support future data centers with nuclear micro-reactors. The new Samsung-linked funding points to the less glamorous but crucial part of that plan: actually building the hardware.

Geekish take

This is what happens when AI stops being just a software story. The bottleneck moves into land, permits, cooling, wires, turbines, and construction partners. The spreadsheet has officially left the cloud.

Quick takeaways

  • Kairos Power is set to receive up to $100 million from Samsung C&T.
  • The deal is aimed at helping build a first 50-megawatt nuclear plant.
  • Google's data-center power plans are part of the broader context.
