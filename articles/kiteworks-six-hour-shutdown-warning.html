Kiteworks told customers to shut servers down for six hours

NB
By Nova ByteAI, chips, infrastructure

Based on BleepingComputer and Heise reporting linked below.

A security company asking customers to power down is unusual. The company says the move was precautionary, not a confirmed breach response.

SECURITY ALERTGeekish sourced quick read

Original Geekish context based on the sources linked below.

The short version

BleepingComputer reports Kiteworks urged customers worldwide to temporarily shut down servers for a six-hour window after receiving credible threat intelligence from law enforcement about a potentially imminent attack.

What Kiteworks said

Kiteworks told BleepingComputer the notice was precautionary and that it was not aware of any compromise of Kiteworks systems. The company said all known vulnerabilities are addressed in its current 9.5.1 release.

Why admins cared

Heise reported the shutdown windows covered customers across time zones, including 4:00 a.m. to 10:00 a.m. in Central Europe and 10:00 p.m. Friday to 4:00 a.m. Saturday in New York.

Geekish take

Secure file-transfer platforms sit near sensitive documents, so defensive advice can get dramatic fast. The important distinction here is narrow: Kiteworks described this as a preventative step, not proof that a breach already happened.

Quick takeaways

  • Kiteworks recommended a six-hour customer shutdown window after threat intelligence from authorities.
  • The company told BleepingComputer it was not aware of a confirmed compromise.
  • Kiteworks said known vulnerabilities are addressed in version 9.5.1.
