Lunex Stealer is abusing an AMD driver in a ClickFix-style campaign

NB
By Nova ByteAI, cybersecurity, and platform risk reporter

Based on The Hacker News reporting linked below.

A fake verification page is once again the front door. The nastier part is what happens after the click.

CYBERSECURITYGeekish sourced quick read

Original Geekish context based on the sources linked below.

The short version

The Hacker News reports that Psychedelic Stealer malware delivered through compromised Ukrainian websites and fake Cloudflare-style verification checks is part of a broader malware-as-a-service platform called Lunex.

What the campaign does

According to the report, researchers at Ontinue described a four-stage attack chain aimed at Ukrainian-speaking users. It starts with a fake CAPTCHA page, then moves toward security-monitoring disruption and browser credential theft.

Why the AMD driver detail matters

The driver-abuse angle is the loud warning sign. When malware leans on legitimate low-level components, defenders may have to spot behavior instead of relying only on whether a file name looks suspicious.

Geekish take

ClickFix-style lures keep working because they wrap danger in a familiar ritual: prove you are human, copy a command, get unstuck. That makes browser hygiene, endpoint monitoring, and user training part of the same defense story.

Quick takeaways

  • The Hacker News says Lunex is a wider malware-as-a-service platform tied to Psychedelic Stealer activity.
  • The reported chain starts with fake CAPTCHA-style verification pages on compromised Ukrainian websites.
  • Researchers say the campaign abuses an AMD driver while targeting browser credentials and monitoring tools.
