Perseverance is back in update mode with a strange Martian rock

NASA's rover update stream has resumed, and the new dispatch points to ancient terrain, stubborn rocks, and a science team still getting surprised on Mars.

NB
Updated September 29, 2026

Concise Geekish brief based on NASA's Perseverance mission update.

What happened

NASA says regular Perseverance rover updates are back after a long pause. The new update catches readers up on the rover's 2026 work in the Lac de Charmes area, west of the Jezero crater rim.

The region matters because NASA says its rocks are among the oldest Perseverance has encountered, formed before the river and lake sediments inside Jezero crater. That makes them useful for understanding early Mars and the wider story of rocky planets.

The weird rock bit

The rover has made 13 abrasion patches in the area so scientists can inspect fresh rock surfaces. NASA says many appear to be igneous rocks, impactite rocks, or rocks shaped by both processes.

One light-toned boulder at a site nicknamed Idubi revealed an unusual light-and-dark texture in an abrasion patch called Badger Peak. NASA says an attempt to sample it failed because the boulder was too hard, so the team is looking for a similar rock elsewhere.

Quick takeaways

  • Perseverance has spent 2026 exploring ancient terrain west of the Jezero crater rim.
  • A new driving algorithm is helping the rover make longer and more accurate drives.
  • The rover is preparing to move on after more work around Lac de Charmes.
