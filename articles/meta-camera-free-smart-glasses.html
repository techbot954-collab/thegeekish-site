Meta made the camera-free AI glasses official

JP
By Jax Pixelapps, social tech, and internet culture reporter

Based on TechCrunch, The Verge, Engadget, and Meta source material.

The rumor became a product: Ray-Ban Meta Audio drops the camera and turns privacy into part of the smart-glasses pitch.

CAMERA-FREE AI GLASSESGeekish sourced quick read

Original Geekish context based on the sources linked below.

The short version

TechCrunch reports Meta announced Ray-Ban Meta Audio at Connect 2026, confirming earlier reports that the company was preparing AI glasses without a camera. The new model focuses on music, podcasts, calls, live translation, and talking to Meta's Muse assistant.

Why this matters

The camera-free design is the point. TechCrunch says the glasses start at $349, weigh 43 grams, and are rated for up to 12 hours of battery life, or up to 48 hours with the charging case. Meta is also pitching 23 color and lens combinations across two Ray-Ban styles.

The business angle

The Verge and Engadget also frame the move as a response to smart-glasses privacy pressure. Meta is still selling camera-equipped models, including Ray-Ban Meta Gen 3, but Ray-Ban Meta Audio gives buyers a less awkward answer when someone asks whether the glasses are recording.

Geekish take

The interesting shift is that "no camera" is no longer just a limitation. For smart glasses, it may be the feature that makes the product socially usable.

Quick takeaways

  • Meta announced Ray-Ban Meta Audio, its first camera-free AI glasses.
  • TechCrunch says they start at $349 and offer up to 12 hours of battery life.
  • The product shifts the pitch from recording to audio, translation, calls, and Muse access.
