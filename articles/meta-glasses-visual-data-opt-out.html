Meta's smart-glasses privacy fix still needs an opt-out

JP
By Jax Pixelapps, social tech, and internet culture reporter

Based on Engadget's Meta smart-glasses privacy reporting.

Meta is answering one of the loudest smart-glasses privacy complaints, but the important detail is that users still have to choose the setting.

SMART GLASSESGeekish sourced quick read

Original Geekish context based on the source linked below.

The short version

Engadget reports Meta will let smart-glasses users opt out of having "visual data" from camera-based Meta AI interactions used to train the company's AI models.

What changes

If a user opts out, Meta says visual data used for a camera-based request, like translating a menu or identifying a sign, will be used to answer that request and then deleted. Engadget says those images also will not be shown to third-party contractors for product improvement or AI training review.

What does not change

The setting is not a total smart-glasses privacy switch. Engadget notes the opt-out covers visual data, while audio interactions with Meta AI are still available for training and review unless users manually delete recordings.

Geekish take

This is a useful correction, but it also shows how messy wearable AI privacy is becoming. A pair of glasses can feel casual, but "ask the camera a question" is still a data pipeline. The setting matters because the default experience shapes what most people actually consent to.

Quick takeaways

  • Engadget reports Meta is adding a visual-data training opt-out for smart glasses.
  • The opt-out applies to camera-based Meta AI interactions, not all AI data.
  • Audio interactions remain a separate privacy issue, according to the report.
