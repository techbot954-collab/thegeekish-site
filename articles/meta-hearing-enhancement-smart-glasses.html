Meta is turning smart glasses into hearing helpers

JP
By Jax Pixelapps, social tech, and internet culture reporter

Based on Engadget's Connect 2026 coverage and Meta's AI glasses product material.

Meta's glasses story is not only cameras and AI agents now. Engadget reports the company is adding FDA-cleared hearing enhancement to its smart-glasses lineup.

ACCESSIBLE WEARABLESGeekish sourced quick read

Original Geekish context based on the sources linked below.

The short version

Engadget reports Meta is bringing FDA-cleared hearing enhancement to its smart glasses. The feature is aimed at adults with perceived mild to moderate hearing loss and will be offered with a free trial before costing $150 as an add-on or requiring a Meta One subscription.

Why it matters

This turns smart glasses into more than a camera-or-assistant gadget. Hearing help gives the wearable a practical reason to exist in noisy restaurants, family rooms, transit stops, and everyday conversations where people may want help hearing without switching to a dedicated device.

The fine print

The phrase "FDA-cleared software" matters, but it does not mean the glasses replace a medical diagnosis or every hearing device. The useful question is whether Meta can make the feature easy enough for casual use while being clear about who it is for.

Geekish take

Meta's most convincing smart-glasses pitch may be less about recording the world and more about improving how people move through it. Hearing enhancement is a quieter feature than AI demos, but it might be easier to explain to normal humans.

Quick takeaways

  • Engadget says Meta is adding FDA-cleared hearing enhancement to smart glasses.
  • The feature targets perceived mild to moderate hearing loss in adults.
  • After a free trial, Engadget says it will cost $150 or require Meta One.
