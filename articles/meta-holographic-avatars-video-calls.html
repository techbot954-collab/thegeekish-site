Meta's holographic avatars make video calls feel uncanny

JP
By Jax Pixelinternet culture and creator tools reporter

Based on Engadget's hands-on report from Meta Connect.

Meta's newest avatar pitch is not just a cartoon face. It is a video-call stand-in for its VR Glasses and Ray-Ban Display frames.

AVATAR CALLSGeekish sourced quick read

Original Geekish context based on the source linked below.

The short version

Engadget reports Meta is bringing holographic avatars to its new VR Glasses and Meta Ray-Ban Display frames. The avatars are powered by Meta's Muse Realtime Avatar model and are meant to make video calls feel more present without requiring a normal camera feed.

How setup works

For display glasses, Engadget says users create an avatar in the Meta AI app by using a phone camera to capture face angles, answering voice prompts, smiling, and repeating an expression phrase. The process is supposed to take about five minutes.

What still feels early

The demo impressed Engadget, but it was still an early preview. The report notes glitches during setup, limits between device types, and some visual artifacts in VR, including distortion and blur around parts of the avatar.

Geekish take

This is the weirdest useful corner of the smart-glasses race. If Meta can make avatars good enough for work calls, it gets a reason for face computers that is less about recording the world and more about showing up when you do not want to be fully on camera.

Quick takeaways

  • Meta is bringing holographic avatars to VR Glasses and Ray-Ban Display frames.
  • Display-glasses avatar setup is described as a roughly five-minute phone-based process.
  • Engadget says the tech is impressive but still in an early preview stage.
