Meta's Muse AI gadget push is getting an open-source maker layer

Muse Gadgets lets builders connect Meta's Muse to small displays, boards, buttons, sensors, and other workbench hardware.

NB
Updated October 2, 2026

Concise Geekish brief based on Muse Gadgets, GitHub, and The Verge.

What happened

Meta has published a Muse Gadget SDK on GitHub and a Muse Gadgets site for builders. The site describes Muse gadgets as open-source devices you build yourself, using off-the-shelf ESP32 boards or Raspberry Pi setups connected to displays, buttons, sensors, actuators, and other hardware.

The Verge reports that Meta's examples include putting Muse on a color E Ink display for reminders, using an HDMI stick to show Muse on a bigger screen, or building small touchscreen devices around the assistant.

Why it matters

The AI hardware conversation has mostly been about polished consumer devices. Muse Gadgets points in the opposite direction: let hobbyists and developers wire the assistant into physical interfaces first, then see what sticks.

That could make Muse feel less like a single product and more like a platform experiment for ambient displays, home controls, and desk gadgets. It also puts privacy and permission questions closer to the hardware layer.

Quick takeaways

  • The SDK targets maker hardware including ESP32 boards and Raspberry Pi setups.
  • Muse Gadgets frames the devices as open source and builder-owned.
  • The move turns AI assistants into a physical-computing playground, not just an app feature.
