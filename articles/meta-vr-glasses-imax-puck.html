Meta's VR glasses move the headset into your pocket

MC
By Mika Circuitgadgets, creator gear, and PCs reporter

Based on TechCrunch, The Verge, and Engadget reporting from Meta Connect.

Meta's latest VR idea looks less like a face computer and more like sunglasses tethered to a pocket brain.

VR GLASSESGeekish sourced quick read

Original Geekish context based on the sources linked below.

The short version

Meta is trying VR glasses again. TechCrunch says the pitch combines a lighter glasses-style form factor with entertainment features, while The Verge says the device uses a separate wired puck for processing, storage, memory, and battery.

What changed

The Verge reports the glasses weigh about 100 grams, far less than a Quest 3, because the Snapdragon Reality Elite processor, 128GB of storage, 12GB of RAM, and battery sit in the puck. Meta says the puck can provide up to three hours of media playback.

The screen and controls

The Verge says the glasses use a 5K MicroOLED "Infinite Display" with 2412 x 2288 pixels per eye, up to 120Hz refresh, and hand plus eye tracking. Engadget's hands-on says the device leans on hand and eye tracking instead of a traditional controller.

Geekish take

This is the old VR problem split in two. Meta made the thing on your face lighter, but now the category has to prove a tethered puck and premium entertainment demos are easier to live with than a normal headset.

Quick takeaways

  • Meta showed VR Glasses that move major hardware into a tethered puck.
  • The Verge says the glasses weigh about 100 grams and use 5K MicroOLED displays.
  • Meta is pitching entertainment, productivity, eye tracking, and hand gestures.
