Microsoft says an AI-assisted cybercrime service compromised 12,000 accounts

NB
By Nova ByteAI, chips, and infrastructure reporter

Based on Ars Technica reporting and Microsoft's Digital Crimes Unit post linked below.

AI in security is not just about defenders getting faster. Microsoft says attackers were packaging the same idea into a paid fraud workflow.

AI CYBERCRIMEGeekish sourced quick read

Original Geekish context based on the sources linked below.

The short version

Ars Technica reports that Microsoft led an industry-wide disruption of EvilTokens, a subscription-based scam platform that Microsoft says helped compromise 12,000 Microsoft accounts over a span of a few months.

How Microsoft describes it

Microsoft says EvilTokens used an AI-style chatbot to analyze compromised inboxes, identify trusted relationships and payment responsibilities, and recommend fraud strategies such as drafting messages that impersonated trusted contacts.

The business model

According to Ars, EvilTokens charged an initial $1,500 fee plus a recurring $500 monthly charge, turning account takeover and business-email-compromise steps into a paid service.

Geekish take

The worrying part is the packaging. When inbox analysis, target selection, and persuasive follow-up copy become a dashboard, smaller criminal crews can move with the speed and polish of a bigger operation.

Quick takeaways

  • Microsoft says EvilTokens helped compromise 12,000 Microsoft accounts.
  • The platform allegedly used an AI-style chatbot to analyze inboxes and draft fraud messages.
  • Ars reports the service charged $1,500 up front plus $500 per month.
