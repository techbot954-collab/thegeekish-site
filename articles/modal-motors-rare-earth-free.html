Modal Motors wants electric motors without rare-earth magnets

MC
By Mika Circuitgadgets, creator gear, and PCs reporter

Based on TechCrunch's Startup Battlefield 200 report.

The startup is chasing a nerdy but important hardware problem: making small, light motors without leaning on rare-earth magnet supply chains.

HARDWAREGeekish sourced quick read

Original Geekish context based on the sources linked below.

The short version

TechCrunch reports that Modal Motors is developing small, light electric motors that do not use rare-earth magnets. The company is pitching the motors for hardware categories like drones, fans, and robots.

Why that is interesting

Rare-earth magnets are common in efficient motors, but they create supply-chain and sourcing pressure. A working alternative could matter most in the smaller machines that are multiplying fast: delivery drones, warehouse robots, home robots, and compact industrial systems.

What to watch

The big question is not just whether Modal can make a clever motor. It is whether the design can scale, hit the right price, and perform well enough for manufacturers to swap away from familiar magnet-based parts.

Geekish take

This is the kind of startup story that hides in the parts bin but could shape future gadgets. If robots and drones are everywhere, the boring components become strategic.

Quick takeaways

  • TechCrunch says Modal Motors is building rare-earth-free electric motors.
  • The target use cases include drones, fans, and robots.
  • The pitch is about reducing dependence on rare-earth magnet supply chains.
