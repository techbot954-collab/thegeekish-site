Modulate is chasing the messier side of voice AI

The startup's new funding is aimed at models that listen for deepfakes, scams, intent, emotion, and whether voice agents are following the rules.

NB
Updated September 28, 2026

Concise Geekish brief based on TechCrunch reporting.

What happened

TechCrunch reports that Boston-based voice intelligence startup Modulate raised $25 million for a platform that uses smaller models to support transcription, emotional analysis, deepfake and AI music detection, and policy enforcement for voice agents in regulated industries.

The report says Modulate runs more than 100 models across signal extraction and analysis/detection. Those models look at vocal emotion, tone, language, whether a voice is synthetic, caller intent, rule violations, and scam behavior.

Why Geekish readers should care

Voice AI usually gets attention when it sounds more human. Modulate is working the other half of the loop: what enterprises need to know once synthetic and human voices are mixed into customer support, compliance, fraud, and cyberattack workflows.

That makes the startup part of a bigger voice-AI pattern. As generation gets cheaper and more convincing, detection, audit trails, and on-prem or on-device privacy options become less optional.

Quick takeaways

  • TechCrunch says the new round was led by Future Ventures, with Hyperplane and Lakestar participating.
  • Modulate specializes in detecting deepfakes and warning call centers about possible scams.
  • The company is working on more on-premises and on-device deployment options for privacy.
