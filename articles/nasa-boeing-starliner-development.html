NASA is giving Starliner another path back to crew flight

The agency says it intends to add Starliner missions while Boeing works through fixes, another test flight, and a new rocket certification path.

JP
Updated September 29, 2026

Concise Geekish brief based on NASA and Ars Technica reporting.

What happened

NASA and Boeing shared new Starliner development plans on Monday. NASA says it intends to exercise options for fifth and sixth Starliner flights to and from the International Space Station, while also working with Boeing and United Launch Alliance to certify ULA's Vulcan rocket for future crew transportation after Atlas V retires.

The immediate plan is an uncrewed Starliner-1 mission to the station, potentially in December 2026 or January 2027. NASA says that flight is meant to validate spacecraft improvements, gather data, and support certification work before a planned return of astronauts on Starliner-2 by 2028.

Why Geekish readers should care

Starliner has become a test of how much patience space agencies and contractors have for redundancy. NASA still wants multiple U.S. crew transportation options, but the next steps are not a simple victory lap: thruster thermal behavior, valve design changes, crew module thrusters, batteries, and parachute tweaks are all part of the path back.

Ars Technica frames the move as part of NASA's attempt to preserve low-Earth-orbit access as older crew systems approach retirement and commercial space station plans move closer.

Quick takeaways

  • NASA intends to add Starliner flights five and six under existing options.
  • Starliner-1 is planned as an uncrewed engineering evaluation mission.
  • NASA's current plan targets astronaut return on Starliner-2 by 2028.
