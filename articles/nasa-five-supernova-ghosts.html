NASA's APOD shows the ghosts of five supernovas

JP
By Jax Pixelapps, social tech, and internet culture reporter

Based on NASA's Astronomy Picture of the Day for September 24.

Today's NASA APOD is a cosmic graveyard: five ancient supernova remnants spread across a huge patch of sky in Auriga.

SPACEGeekish sourced quick read

Original Geekish context based on the source linked below.

The short version

NASA's September 24 Astronomy Picture of the Day features "The Ghosts of Five Supernovas," an image of five supernova remnants in the constellation Auriga. NASA says the view was acquired at Oukaimeden Observatory in Morocco with approximately 200 hours of observations.

Why it is cool

The APOD explanation says the scene covers a patch of sky equivalent to about one thousand full moons tiled together. The remnants are shells of shocked gas and filaments left behind by separate stellar explosions, with estimated ages up to tens of thousands of years.

Geekish take

This is the kind of space image that rewards slow looking. It is not one dramatic explosion; it is the fossil record of several of them, spread across the sky like old fingerprints.

Quick takeaways

  • NASA's APOD for September 24 highlights five supernova remnants in Auriga.
  • The source image is credited by NASA to Stephane Vetter and Yann Sainty.
  • NASA says the image used about 200 hours of observations from Oukaimeden Observatory.
