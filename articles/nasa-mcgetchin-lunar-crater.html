NASA's APOD spotlights a new Moon crater named McGetchin

MC
By Mika Circuitgadgets, creator gear, and PCs reporter

Based on NASA APOD and LRO context linked below.

The Moon looks still from Earth, but NASA's before-and-after imagery is a reminder that it is still getting edited by space rocks.

MOON WATCHGeekish sourced quick read

Original Geekish context based on the sources linked below.

The short version

NASA's September 23 Astronomy Picture of the Day says a comet or asteroid roughly 10 to 20 meters across struck the Moon sometime between April and May 2024, leaving a new crater now named McGetchin.

How NASA saw it

NASA says the Lunar Reconnaissance Orbiter captured before-and-after views through its regular lunar monitoring. The APOD entry says the crater is about two soccer fields across.

The science bit

According to NASA, follow-up thermal imaging found a large cold spot around the warmer crater. That matters because impacts can fluff up lunar regolith, changing how the surface holds heat.

Geekish take

This is exactly why long-running space instruments are quietly powerful. The big discovery is not just a crater; it is having enough baseline imagery to catch the Moon before and after the hit.

Quick takeaways

  • NASA says the impact happened between April and May 2024.
  • The crater is named after Apollo-era lunar scientist McGetchin.
  • NASA credits the APOD imagery to NASA/GSFC/Intuitive Machines.
