NASA's APOD caught a meteor reflected in a giant telescope mirror

MC
By Mika Circuithardware, science, and creator tools reporter

Based on NASA's Astronomy Picture of the Day for September 26.

A Perseid meteor, the Milky Way, and a 23-meter telescope mirror all landed in one excellent sky shot.

Original Geekish context based on the source linked below.

The short version

NASA's September 26 Astronomy Picture of the Day features a Perseid meteor over the Observatorio del Roque de los Muchachos at La Palma, Canary Islands, Spain. The scene also shows the Milky Way and the segmented mirror of the prototype Large-Sized Telescope, LST-1.

Why the mirror matters

NASA says LST-1 has a 23-meter diameter mirror made from 198 hexagonal segments. It is built to detect extremely brief visible-light flashes produced when energetic gamma rays from cosmic sources trigger atmospheric particle showers.

Geekish take

The image works because it stacks two kinds of geek beauty: a natural sky event and a machine designed to catch some of the universe's fastest, faintest signals.

Quick takeaways

  • NASA's APOD shows a Perseid meteor from August 15.
  • The foreground mirror is the LST-1 telescope at La Palma.
  • NASA credits the source image to Jeff Dai of TWAN.
