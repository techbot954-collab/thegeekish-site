NASA may give Starliner a bigger role after all

NB
By Nova ByteAI, chips, and infrastructure reporter

Based on Ars Technica reporting linked below.

Starliner has spent years as the troubled second option in NASA's commercial crew story. Now NASA may be preparing to make that backup option matter more, not less.

COMMERCIAL CREWGeekish sourced quick read

Original Geekish context based on the source linked below.

The short version

Ars Technica reports that NASA could announce as early as next week that it will order two additional crewed Starliner missions from Boeing.

What NASA may do

According to Ars, NASA is also expected to pay some costs to address Starliner's thruster issues and support certification of the spacecraft on a new rocket after Atlas V retires.

Why now

Ars reports the planning comes after SpaceX told NASA officials it intends to retire Crew Dragon by 2030, if not earlier. Crew Dragon is currently NASA's only operational spacecraft for routine astronaut trips to low-Earth orbit.

Geekish take

NASA's commercial crew strategy only works if redundancy is real. Starliner has to prove itself technically, but NASA has a strong reason to avoid being boxed into a single ride to orbit.

Quick takeaways

  • Ars says NASA may order two additional crewed Starliner missions.
  • The plan reportedly includes support for thruster fixes and a new launch vehicle certification path.
  • The move is tied to longer-term access concerns around Crew Dragon's future.
