An IT mistake erased 11 years of maternity-record viewing history

NB
By Nova ByteAI, chips, infrastructure, and security reporter

Based on Ars Technica, Nottingham University Hospitals, and The Register reporting.

The patient-care data was recovered, but the lost audit trail shows why backups are not only about the obvious files.

Original Geekish context based on the sources linked below.

The short version

Ars Technica reports Nottingham University Hospitals NHS Trust lost data from maternity records spanning 11 years after a human error during routine technical work. The trust said patient care data was recovered, but viewing history was lost.

What happened

According to the hospital statement cited by Ars, the issue happened while creating a copy of a radiotherapy database for reporting purposes. The Register also covered the incident, which Ars says affected maternity-record viewing history rather than the recovered patient-care records themselves.

Why it matters

Medical systems do not only need the chart. They also need the audit history that shows who accessed what and when. When that layer disappears, accountability and investigations can get harder even if clinical records are restored.

Geekish take

This is the boring side of tech until it suddenly is not. Backups, permissions, test copies, and audit trails are invisible infrastructure; when one breaks, everyone learns how much was riding on it.

Quick takeaways

  • Ars reports 11 years of maternity-record viewing history was lost.
  • Nottingham University Hospitals attributed the incident to human error during routine technical work.
  • The trust said patient care data was recovered.
