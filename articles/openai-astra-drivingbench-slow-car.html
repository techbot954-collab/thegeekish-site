GPT-6 Astra drove a car, very slowly

MC
By Mika Circuithardware, cars, and practical tech reporter

Based on The Register's report on the DrivingBench road test.

A general AI model completing a real cone course is wild. The details make it less like a robotaxi breakthrough and more like a very expensive lab demo with a brake pedal nearby.

LLM DRIVINGGeekish sourced quick read

Original Geekish context based on the source linked below.

The short version

The Register reports that OpenAI's GPT-6 Astra completed a 134.7-meter DrivingBench course in a Toyota Corolla, finishing on its second attempt in 5 minutes and 22 seconds. That works out to roughly 0.94 mph.

The expensive part

The researchers told The Register the run used about 6.6 million tokens and cost $7.74 in inference, helped by cached input pricing. The setup also used a comma four driver-assistance device, a laptop, a phone, and a human ready to brake.

Why it matters

The result is interesting because the model was not a specialized self-driving stack. It used perception, reasoning, planning, and tool calls to get through a simple physical course. It is also a reminder that latency, safety refusals, network dependence, and cost still make frontier models a poor fit for real driving.

Geekish take

This is the kind of demo that deserves both raised eyebrows and a raised hand on the emergency brake. General AI can sometimes control the world, but specialized systems still win when milliseconds, reliability, and safety cases matter.

Quick takeaways

  • GPT-6 Astra reportedly completed a small real-world cone course at under 1 mph.
  • The run required millions of tokens plus a driver-assist hardware setup.
  • The demo is impressive, but it does not make general LLMs ready to drive on real roads.
