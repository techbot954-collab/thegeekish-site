Peloton's new Tread Flex folds to chase smaller spaces

JP
By Jax PixelApps, social tech, and internet culture reporter

Based on The Verge and Engadget reporting linked below.

Peloton's treadmill refresh is aimed at apartment realities: lower price, fold-up storage, wheels, and software hooks that try to keep the subscription story moving.

CONNECTED FITNESSGeekish sourced quick read

Original Geekish context based on the sources linked below.

The short version

The Verge reports Peloton introduced the Tread Flex, a $2,195 folding treadmill that can stand upright and save 50 percent more floor space. Engadget also described it as Peloton's first fold-up treadmill and its cheapest treadmill to date.

What changed

According to The Verge, the Flex has caster wheels, a Tread Lock passcode, and a two-cylinder release that lowers the deck slowly. It has a swivel screen, but no camera, which means it misses some Peloton IQ features available on camera-enabled models.

The software angle

Peloton is adding features across the treadmill line, including personalized pace targets after about 10 running workouts and ReRun, which can recreate outdoor route elevation on the treadmill from compatible course data.

Geekish take

This is Peloton trying to make its treadmill pitch less mansion-gym and more city-apartment. The Flex still is not cheap, but folding hardware gives the company a clearer answer to the people who like the classes and do not have a dedicated fitness room.

Quick takeaways

  • The Tread Flex is priced at $2,195, according to The Verge.
  • It folds upright and can be moved on caster wheels.
  • Camera-based Peloton IQ features remain tied to higher-end Vision models.
