A new piracy-blocking bill could pull ISPs, DNS, and VPNs into the fight

JP
By Jax Pixelapps, social tech, and internet culture reporter

Based on Ars Technica's policy reporting and the bill text linked below.

A proposed U.S. site-blocking law is the kind of internet policy story that sounds niche until it reaches the pipes people use every day.

INTERNET POLICYGeekish sourced quick read

Original Geekish context based on the sources linked below.

The short version

Ars Technica reports that Rep. Darrell Issa introduced a bill that would create a process for rightsholders to seek court orders blocking foreign piracy sites through ISPs, domain name resolution services, VPNs, and other network operators.

Why it is bigger than piracy

Site-blocking laws are pitched as copyright enforcement, especially for fast-moving live sports streams. The fight is broader because blocking orders can affect DNS resolution, VPN routing, and the way ordinary users reach parts of the web.

What the bill text says

The bill text on Congress.gov frames the target as foreign piracy sites and creates a court-order process. That means the real debate will likely turn on scope, due process, overblocking risk, and how much infrastructure providers should be responsible for enforcement.

Geekish take

Copyright owners want faster tools than takedown notices. Internet infrastructure operators and digital-rights advocates will want guardrails so blocking does not become blunt-force censorship by accident.

Quick takeaways

  • The proposal targets foreign piracy sites through court-ordered blocking.
  • Ars says the bill could reach ISPs, DNS providers, VPNs, and other network operators.
  • The important questions are scope, process, and overblocking risk.
