PNOE's new mask wants breath testing to be self-serve

MC
By Mika CircuitGadgets, EVs, and practical tech reporter

Based on TechCrunch's report on PNOE's new self-serve breath-testing mask.

Lab-style metabolic testing is trying to escape the lab. PNOE's next device is built for gyms, wellness businesses, and eight-minute self-serve sessions.

HEALTH TECHGeekish sourced quick read

Original Geekish context based on the source linked below.

The short version

TechCrunch reports that PNOE is preparing to launch PNOE 2.0 on October 1. The device measures oxygen consumption and carbon dioxide output, and the new version is designed so users can administer an eight-minute test themselves.

Why this matters

Metabolic testing can measure VO2 max, resting metabolic rate, and metabolic flexibility, but it has usually required expensive gear and trained operators. PNOE says a smaller self-serve design could bring the test into more fitness centers and possibly pharmacies.

Geekish take

This is the useful edge of wearable-adjacent health tech: less vague score-chasing, more measurements that can connect to training and nutrition decisions. The caution is just as important: TechCrunch notes PNOE is not FDA-cleared and the company says it does not provide medical recommendations.

Quick takeaways

  • PNOE 2.0 is expected to launch October 1, according to TechCrunch.
  • The self-serve test is described as an eight-minute breathing session.
  • The device is positioned as wellness tech, not a medical diagnostic tool.
