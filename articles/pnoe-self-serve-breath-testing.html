PNOE wants lab-style breath testing to work without the lab ritual

The startup's new mask points at a broader gadget trend: turning clinical measurements into faster, self-guided experiences for gyms, clinics, and wellness programs.

JP
Updated September 28, 2026

Concise Geekish brief based on TechCrunch reporting and PNOE product materials.

What happened

TechCrunch reports that PNOE, a Malden, Massachusetts-based startup, is launching a sleeker self-serve version of its breath-analyzing mask on October 1.

The reported pitch is speed and autonomy: gym-goers could measure VO2 max and other metabolic markers in eight minutes without a trained operator. PNOE's own site describes its platform around VO2 max, resting metabolic rate, metabolic testing, and breath analysis for clinics.

Why it is geeky

Fitness wearables already estimate a lot from wrists. Breath testing is different because it tries to measure oxygen use and metabolism more directly, which is why the workflow usually feels more clinical.

If PNOE can make that experience self-serve, the story is less "another mask gadget" and more a sign that serious health metrics are being repackaged as repeatable consumer-facing sessions.

Quick takeaways

  • TechCrunch says the new self-serve PNOE mask launches October 1.
  • The reported test time is eight minutes for VO2 max and other metabolic markers.
  • The important question is whether convenience can preserve the credibility users expect from lab-style testing.
