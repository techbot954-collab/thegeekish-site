Right-to-repair is turning gadget frustration into a roadshow

MC
By Mika CircuitGadgets, creator gear, and PC reporter

Based on WIRED's report on Louis Rossmann's right-to-repair live tour.

The next device-rights story is not only a policy fight. It is also a culture fight over whether people actually control the hardware they buy.

DEVICE RIGHTSGeekish sourced quick read

Original Geekish context based on the source linked below.

The short version

WIRED reports that YouTuber and repair advocate Louis Rossmann is taking a grassroots live tour on the road, highlighting resistance to companies that keep customers from controlling their own devices.

Why it matters

Right-to-repair used to sound like a niche issue for phone technicians and laptop tinkerers. Now it touches subscriptions, parts pairing, software locks, app access, and the basic question of what ownership means when every object has a cloud account attached.

The Geekish angle

The movement works because it is emotionally simple: people do not like paying full price for a device and then discovering the company still controls what they can fix, replace, unlock, or keep using.

Quick takeaways

  • WIRED frames the tour as part of growing resistance to locked-down devices.
  • Louis Rossmann remains one of the most visible voices in repair advocacy.
  • The issue is expanding from repair shops into broader digital ownership culture.
