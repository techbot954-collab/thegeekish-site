Roku wants your TV to become an app lab

JP
By Jax Pixelapps, social tech, and internet culture reporter

Based on TechCrunch's report on the latest Roku OS update.

Roku Labs is a new home-screen hub for experimental apps. It starts with weather, stocks, and a Roku City game, but the bigger signal is Roku treating the TV like a first-party software surface.

CONNECTED TVGeekish sourced quick read

Original Geekish context based on the source linked below.

The short version

TechCrunch reports that Roku's latest OS update introduces Roku Labs, a new tile on the Home Screen where U.S. customers can try experimental Roku-made apps.

What's inside

The first Labs apps include weather, stocks, and Roku Dash, a game where players fly a UFO across Roku City, the company's familiar neon screensaver world. Roku Voice is also getting weather answers.

The platform play

TechCrunch frames the move as Roku increasing its investment in first-party apps that could become monetizable. Roku is also expanding personalized home screens to Mexico and Brazil, adding smaller-service subscription bundles, and open-sourcing Roku LT OS for its remote software.

Geekish take

The TV home screen is not just a launcher anymore. It is becoming a testing ground for games, utilities, bundles, ads, and eventually smarter assistants. Roku Labs is tiny, but it shows where the big-screen interface is headed.

Quick takeaways

  • Roku Labs gives U.S. users experimental Roku-made apps from the Home Screen.
  • Early apps include weather, stocks, and the Roku Dash UFO game.
  • The update also brings subscription bundles and broader personalized screens.
