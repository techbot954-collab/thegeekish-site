Ryuk ransomware access specialist gets 24 months

NB
By Nova ByteAI, chips, and infrastructure reporter

Based on BleepingComputer's sentencing report and the DOJ's earlier plea announcement.

The case is a reminder that ransomware crews are not just malware operators. Initial access is its own business line.

Original Geekish context based on the sources linked below.

The short version

BleepingComputer reports that Karen Serobovich Vardanyan, also known online as "Maneeken" or "Karl Lagerfeld," was sentenced to 24 months in prison and three years of supervised release for his role in Ryuk ransomware attacks on U.S. organizations.

What prosecutors had alleged

In its July plea announcement, the U.S. Department of Justice said Vardanyan and co-conspirators illegally accessed victim networks and deployed ransomware on hundreds of compromised servers and workstations. Prosecutors said the group received about 1,610 bitcoins in ransom payments, valued at more than $15 million at the time.

Why Ryuk still matters

Ryuk was active from 2018 into 2020 and became notorious for attacks on health care and other organizations during the COVID-19 period. BleepingComputer notes that the crew behind Ryuk later shifted into Conti, which then splintered after leaks and shutdowns.

Geekish take

Ransomware stories often focus on payloads, but the first foothold is where the business starts. This sentence is about that access layer: the logins, exposed systems, and quiet compromises that make the loud ransom note possible.

Quick takeaways

  • Vardanyan was sentenced to 24 months in prison, according to BleepingComputer.
  • The DOJ previously said the conspiracy deployed ransomware on hundreds of systems.
  • The Ryuk ecosystem fed into later ransomware operations, including Conti.
