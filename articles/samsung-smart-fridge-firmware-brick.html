Samsung smart fridges show the risk of firmware updates

MC
By Mika Circuitgadgets, creator gear, and PCs reporter

Based on Ars Technica's report and the Korean outlet cited in it.

Smart appliances sound convenient until an update goes wrong. This time, the reported fallout was not a frozen app. It was food that could spoil.

SMART HOMEGeekish sourced quick read

Original Geekish context based on the sources linked below.

The short version

Ars Technica reports that some Samsung Bespoke AI refrigerators stopped working after a SmartThings firmware update. According to the report, affected fridges lost power or became stuck during the update process, while owners saw the devices appear offline in the SmartThings app.

Why it matters

A phone update can be annoying. A fridge update can become an expensive household problem. Ars says some owners described spoiled food after the failure, which turns the familiar software-update headache into a reminder that connected appliances are still appliances first.

What is still developing

The early reporting points to Samsung's Bespoke AI line, especially newer four-door models cited by Korean outlet Star News Korea. The exact scope, repair path, and whether every affected unit shares the same update failure still need clearer public confirmation.

Geekish take

The smart-home promise is comfort with less friction. But when basic household hardware depends on cloud apps and firmware pipelines, reliability matters more than novelty. A refrigerator should not feel like a beta device.

Quick takeaways

  • Ars reports some Samsung Bespoke AI fridges stopped working after a SmartThings firmware update.
  • Owners reportedly saw fridges go offline or get stuck on an update message.
  • The story highlights the stakes of software failures in essential home appliances.
