Shower steam is not the same as phone water resistance

MC
By Mika Circuithardware, science, and creator tools reporter

Based on Engadget's current phone-care explainer and manufacturer guidance it cites.

An IP rating is not a backstage pass for hot showers, steam rooms, and slow battery damage.

PHONE CAREGeekish sourced quick read

Original Geekish context based on the source linked below.

The short version

Engadget says IP67 and IP68 phones are water-resistant, not waterproof, and those ratings do not mean hot steam and humid rooms are harmless.

Why steam is different

Steam can condense into liquid around ports, speakers, buttons, and seals. Engadget also notes that heat and humidity can wear down the protections that keep liquid out and can accelerate battery aging.

Manufacturer guidance is blunt

Engadget points to Apple support guidance telling users to avoid saunas and steam rooms, and Google support guidance that recommends using a Bluetooth speaker instead of bringing the phone into the shower environment.

Geekish take

The easy rule is boring but useful: let the phone sit in the next room and let a speaker do the risky bathroom work. Phones are expensive. Shower playlists are not worth a slow-motion port problem.

Quick takeaways

  • IP ratings are about specific water-resistance tests, not every wet scenario.
  • Steam, heat, and condensation can stress seals, ports, and batteries.
  • Apple and Google both warn against steam-room style exposure.
