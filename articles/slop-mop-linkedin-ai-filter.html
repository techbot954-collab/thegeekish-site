Slop Mop wants to clean up LinkedIn's AI-ish posts

JP
By Jax Pixelapps, social tech, and internet culture reporter

Based on The Register's report on Slop Mop and Jev.

The extension's pitch is simple: stop arguing over whether AI wrote the post and ask whether the post is worth your attention.

FEED FILTERSGeekish sourced quick read

Original Geekish context based on the source linked below.

The short version

The Register reports that Tom Frazier released Slop Mop, a free open-source Chrome extension for LinkedIn that flags or hides low-value posts. It uses Jev, a probabilistic decision AI model, rather than a normal chatbot interface.

How it judges posts

The extension looks for signals such as hype language, engagement bait, suspiciously neat lessons, stiff wording, empty praise, and posts that make readers expand after one line. It also asks whether the writing sounds like a specific person and whether it contains anything useful.

Not just an AI detector

Frazier told The Register the point is not to punish AI writing specifically. Slop Mop is designed to flag bad human writing and bad AI writing equally, while allowing that AI-assisted content can still be useful.

Geekish take

This is a more honest filter than the usual AI detector drama. The feed problem is not just machines writing posts. It is low-effort content optimized to hijack attention. A "worth reading?" layer is exactly the kind of small internet tool people will understand instantly.

Quick takeaways

  • Slop Mop is a free open-source Chrome extension for filtering LinkedIn posts.
  • It uses Jev to score low-value content patterns rather than acting as a pure AI detector.
  • The bigger idea is filtering attention waste, whether a human or AI produced it.
