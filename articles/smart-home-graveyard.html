The smart-home graveyard is a warning label for cloud gadgets

The shutdown of June Oven's app and cloud services shows why a gadget that depends entirely on remote servers can become much less smart overnight.

JP
Updated September 28, 2026

Concise Geekish brief based on The Verge's smart-home shutdown column.

What happened

The Verge reports that Weber, which bought June in 2021 and stopped making June ovens in 2022, shut off the June Oven app and cloud services. The oven still works as an oven, but app control, software updates, support, parts, and AI food-recognition features are gone.

The column places June alongside other connected-device shutdowns, including Nest Secure, early Nest Thermostats, Belkin's WeMo line, Neato robot vacuums, Brava smart ovens, Logitech Pop buttons, Bose SoundTouch speakers, and Sengled smart bulbs.

The Geekish bit

The hardware lesson is simple and brutal: cloud dependence is a product feature until the business case changes. Local APIs, physical controls, and standards like Matter, Thread, Zigbee, Z-Wave, and HomeKit can help devices degrade gracefully when a company moves on.

The Verge also notes that some products have been rescued, such as Insteon users buying the company and Bose open-sourcing its SoundTouch API. Those are exceptions, which is why buyers should treat offline operation as a survival feature.

Quick takeaways

  • June Oven owners lost cloud-backed smart features, though the appliance can still heat food.
  • The broader risk is any connected gadget that has no useful local fallback.
  • Local control and physical controls are not nostalgia; they are resilience.
