SmartLabels turns the junk drawer into an AI inventory test

By Mika CircuitGadgets, creator gear, and PCs reporter

Based on WIRED's review linked below.

The best smart-home tests are the boring ones. Can a label system remember what is in the box after real life makes the box weird?

SMART HOMEGeekish sourced quick read

Original Geekish context based on the source linked below.

The short version

WIRED reviewed SmartLabels, a QR code organizing system that can inventory objects from a photo. The reviewer tested it on a junk drawer and keepsakes to see whether AI-assisted storage could stay useful in a busy household.

Why it caught our eye

AI organization tools often look clean in demos because the objects are clean. A junk drawer is a better test: mixed cables, tiny items, forgotten accessories, and the kind of clutter people actually want help with.

Geekish take

This is a practical little category to watch. If photo-based inventory gets accurate and low-friction enough, QR labels could move from "neat idea" to genuinely useful for storage bins, camera gear, kid stuff, and move-day chaos.

Quick takeaways

  • WIRED tested SmartLabels on real household clutter.
  • The system uses QR labels plus AI-assisted inventory from photos.
  • The big question is whether the database stays easier than simply opening the drawer.
