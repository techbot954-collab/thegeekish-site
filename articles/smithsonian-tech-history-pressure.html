The Smithsonian fight is also a tech-history story

A report on pressure against the Smithsonian shows how science exhibits can become battlegrounds over what tech history is allowed to say.

JP
Updated September 28, 2026

Concise Geekish brief based on Ars Technica's ProPublica-syndicated report.

What happened

Ars Technica, syndicating ProPublica reporting, says the Smithsonian's National Museum of American History became part of a broader political fight over museum narratives. One example cited was the museum's "The Electric Dr. Franklin" exhibit, which connected Benjamin Franklin's electrical-science work with the fact that he enslaved people earlier in life.

The exhibit closed in 2025 but remains available online, and the report says it was among many details criticized in a White House report arguing that the museum had shifted away from straightforward historical education.

Why Geekish readers should care

Tech history is not just old machines in glass cases. The way museums explain invention, labor, funding, and harm affects how people understand science itself.

That makes the Smithsonian dispute relevant beyond politics: it is also about who gets to frame the messy origin stories behind the tools, systems, and scientific icons that geek culture celebrates.

Quick takeaways

  • The story centers on pressure against Smithsonian museum narratives.
  • A Benjamin Franklin electrical-science exhibit was one cited flashpoint.
  • The broader issue is how institutions present science history when the history is complicated.
