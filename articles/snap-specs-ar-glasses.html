Snap’s new Specs are a $2,200 bet on social AR

MC
By Mika Circuitgadgets, PCs, and practical tech reporter

Based on The Verge’s September 16 hands-on and launch coverage.

Snap is taking another run at augmented-reality glasses, this time with consumer Specs and an AI assistant meant to follow you across devices.

AR WEARABLESGeekish sourced quick read

Original Geekish context based on the sources linked below.

The short version

Snap has introduced a new consumer version of Specs, its augmented-reality glasses. The Verge reports the glasses cost $2,200 and showed off shared AR moments, including a dominoes demo where two people wearing Specs could interact with the same virtual objects on a real table.

The AI angle

Snap is also launching Specs Intelligence, an AI assistant that The Verge says can connect to digital accounts, help with tasks, track travel information, and answer chat-style prompts. Snap describes the service as “anticipatory,” which is tech-industry language for assistants that try to act before you explicitly ask.

Why this matters

AR glasses keep circling the same question: can they become useful enough to justify wearing a computer on your face? Snap’s pitch is less “private headset” and more “shared digital layer,” which is a smart fit for a social company. The price, battery life, comfort, app support, and privacy norms will decide whether that idea gets past demos.

Geekish take

The most interesting part is not only the hardware. It is Snap trying to make AR feel social instead of solitary. If Specs can turn mixed reality into something two people actually do together, that is a cleaner story than another floating notification screen.

Quick takeaways

  • Snap is launching consumer AR Specs priced at $2,200, according to The Verge.
  • Specs Intelligence brings an AI assistant to the glasses, iOS, and Mac.
  • The real test is whether shared AR feels useful outside controlled demos.
