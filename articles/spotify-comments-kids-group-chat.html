Kids turned a quiet podcast comment section into a group chat

JP
By Jax PixelApps, social tech, and internet culture reporter

Based on The Verge reporting and the This American Life episode linked below.

When the obvious apps are blocked, kids route around the system. This time, the workaround was the comment section under an NPR podcast.

INTERNET CULTUREGeekish sourced quick read

Original Geekish context based on the sources linked below.

The short version

The Verge reports middle schoolers turned the Spotify comment section under an NPR Wild Card podcast episode into an impromptu group chat, likely because other apps and social networks were blocked.

How they found it

In a new This American Life episode, Ira Glass looked into the comment swarm. The Verge quotes one student saying they picked the podcast because it did not have many comments.

Why this is very internet

The funny part is the venue. The serious part is the behavior: young users will look for any writable surface that still works, even if it is attached to a public-radio interview show.

Geekish take

Every platform feature becomes social infrastructure if enough people need it. Spotify probably did not imagine podcast comments as a school-day backchannel, but that is exactly the kind of weird workaround the web keeps producing.

Quick takeaways

  • Middle schoolers reportedly used Spotify podcast comments as a group chat.
  • The comment thread formed under an NPR Wild Card episode.
  • The story is a reminder that users repurpose any open comment box when normal channels are blocked.
