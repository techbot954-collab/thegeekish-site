Fake songs are showing up on real artist pages

JP
By Jax Pixelapps, social tech, and internet culture reporter

Based on same-day reporting from Engadget.

A distribution loophole has reportedly let scammers attach fake tracks to real artists' profiles, turning music identity into another platform trust problem.

STREAMING TRUSTGeekish sourced quick read

Original Geekish context based on the source linked below.

The short version

Engadget reports that scammers have been inserting fake songs onto real artists' pages on Spotify and other platforms. The report says a distribution loophole lets uploaders collect royalties by pushing fake songs onto profiles for real bands.

Why this matters

Music platforms are built on identity: fans follow an artist page because they trust the name. If unrelated uploads can slip into that surface, the problem is not just spam. It is a direct hit to artist reputation, listener trust, and royalty flows.

The platform lesson

Distribution tools made uploading music easier. Now platforms have to prove they can keep that pipeline from being abused at the artist-page level.

Geekish take

This is the streaming version of account impersonation. The profile page is the product, and verification has to travel with every new upload.

Quick takeaways

  • Engadget says fake songs have appeared on real artist pages.
  • The alleged loophole involves distribution and royalty collection.
  • Artist identity is becoming a streaming security issue.
