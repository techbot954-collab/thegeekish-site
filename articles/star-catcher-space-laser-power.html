Space lasers are moving from sci-fi to power grid test

Star Catcher is preparing to test whether one satellite can beam usable power to another untethered spacecraft in orbit.

JP
Updated September 28, 2026

Concise Geekish brief based on WIRED reporting and linked space-power context.

What happened

WIRED reports that Star Catcher, a Jacksonville-based startup, is preparing to launch a prototype system on a SpaceX rocket this week. The goal is to beam energy by laser from one spacecraft to another satellite in orbit.

The demo, called Protostar in WIRED's report, is meant to test tracking and received power as an untethered cubesat moves away. Star Catcher says its longer-term plan is a network of orbital power nodes that collect sunlight and transmit energy to satellites.

Why it matters

Satellites usually bring their own solar panels and batteries, which limits uptime, weight budgets, and what instruments they can carry. If power can be delivered on demand, the economics of telecom satellites, research payloads, and future orbital compute could change.

It is still early. WIRED notes hard problems around efficiency, thermal management, targeting, and long-term operation in space. But the test is one of those rare geek-culture moments where the phrase "space laser power grid" is not just a joke.

Quick takeaways

  • WIRED says a successful demo would be the first laser power transfer between two untethered objects in space.
  • Star Catcher has announced funding and Space Force support, according to WIRED.
  • The same launch is expected to carry Google's first space-data-center attempt, according to the report.
