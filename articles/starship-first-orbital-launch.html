Starship's first orbital try is a giant rocket story with a clock on it

Space.com says SpaceX's Starship Flight 14 is scheduled for a September 28 attempt that would send the megarocket to orbit for the first time.

NB
Updated September 28, 2026

Concise Geekish brief based on Space.com reporting and SpaceX launch materials.

What happened

Space.com reports that SpaceX's Starship megarocket is scheduled to attempt its first orbital launch on Monday, September 28, with liftoff targeted for 8:15 a.m. EDT, or 12:15 UTC.

The article frames the mission as Starship Flight 14 and says it would be the first time the full Starship system attempts to reach orbit, moving the test campaign beyond high-altitude and suborbital milestones.

Why geeks care

Starship is not just another launch vehicle. It is SpaceX's fully reusable heavy-lift architecture, pairing a Super Heavy booster with an upper-stage spacecraft designed around high flight cadence and large payload capacity.

That makes an orbital attempt a useful reality check. The question is less whether the vehicle looks cinematic and more whether the system can start behaving like repeatable infrastructure.

Quick takeaways

  • The source story was updated before launch and describes the event as a scheduled attempt, not a completed result.
  • Space.com lists the target liftoff time as 8:15 a.m. EDT / 12:15 UTC on September 28.
  • The core geek angle is operational: orbit is where the reusable-rocket promise meets real mission discipline.
