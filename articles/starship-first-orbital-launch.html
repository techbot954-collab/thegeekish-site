Starship reaching orbit turns the megarocket story into an operations story

TechCrunch reports that SpaceX's Starship reached Earth orbit for the first time, even after losing one of the upper stage's six Raptor engines.

NB
Updated September 28, 2026

Concise Geekish brief based on TechCrunch reporting, earlier Space.com preview coverage, and SpaceX launch information.

What happened

TechCrunch reports that SpaceX's Starship reached Earth orbit for the first time after launching from South Texas on Monday morning.

The flight was not spotless. According to TechCrunch, the upper stage lost one of its six Raptor engines after separation. SpaceX initially called off the orbital attempt, then decided minutes later to keep pushing toward orbit.

Why geeks care

Starship is not just another launch vehicle. It is SpaceX's fully reusable heavy-lift architecture, pairing a Super Heavy booster with an upper-stage spacecraft designed around high flight cadence and large payload capacity.

That makes orbit a serious marker. The next questions are whether SpaceX can repeat the feat, deploy payloads consistently, and turn booster recovery from simulated water landings into routine pad operations.

Quick takeaways

  • TechCrunch says Starship reached orbit even after losing one upper-stage Raptor engine.
  • The Super Heavy booster performed what TechCrunch described as the cleanest simulated water landing yet for the V3 Starship version.
  • SpaceX had planned to deploy 26 third-generation Starlink satellites, but TechCrunch said it was not immediately clear whether that plan changed after the delayed entry into orbit.
