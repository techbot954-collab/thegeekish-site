Supabase apps are exposing sensitive data through misconfigured projects

NB
By Nova ByteAI, chips, and infrastructure reporter

Based on TechCrunch reporting and UpGuard research linked below.

The vibe-coded app boom has a very old security problem attached: public databases with private data inside.

APP SECURITYGeekish sourced quick read

Original Geekish context based on the sources linked below.

The short version

TechCrunch reports cybersecurity firm UpGuard found around 16,000 Supabase-hosted databases where some degree of personal data was exposed to the public web. The exposed data reportedly included names, addresses, phone numbers, and a smaller number of passwords and authentication tokens.

Why this keeps happening

The issue is not described as a single Supabase breach. TechCrunch frames it as a shared-responsibility problem where customers can misconfigure projects, especially as AI-assisted and vibe-coded apps move quickly from prototype to public product.

What Supabase says

Supabase CISO Bil Harmer told TechCrunch the company's projects are "secure by default" and that customers control how their projects are configured. He said Supabase notifies affected customers when security issues are discovered.

Geekish take

The warning is not "do not use Supabase." It is that building an app in an afternoon does not remove the boring parts: row-level security, access policies, secrets handling, and checking what the public internet can actually read.

Quick takeaways

  • UpGuard told TechCrunch it found roughly 16,000 Supabase-hosted databases with some exposed personal data.
  • The story points to misconfiguration risk around fast-built apps and AI-assisted development.
  • Supabase says its projects are secure by default and that security is shared with customers.
