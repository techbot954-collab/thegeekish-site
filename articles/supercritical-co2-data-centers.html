Supercritical CO2 is the latest data-center power pitch

NB
By Nova ByteAI, chips, and infrastructure reporter

Based on WIRED's September 23 reporting.

AI infrastructure is turning energy plumbing into headline tech. This one is about squeezing more work out of gas turbines.

AI INFRASTRUCTUREGeekish sourced quick read

Original Geekish context based on the source linked below.

The short version

WIRED reports a startup wants to power data centers using supercritical carbon dioxide technology that can strap onto existing gas turbines and make them more efficient. The important caveat: WIRED says it would not eliminate their emissions entirely.

Why it matters

Data centers need more power, faster than many grids can comfortably supply it. That demand is pushing companies to consider everything from nuclear deals to new turbine add-ons and cleaner generation experiments.

The tradeoff

Efficiency gains can matter if they reduce fuel use for the same computing load. But the climate math still depends on what fuel is burned, how much carbon remains, and whether the tech delays cleaner alternatives.

Geekish take

The next era of AI will be judged as much by substations, turbines, water, and permitting as by benchmark charts. Energy infrastructure is now part of the product story.

Quick takeaways

  • WIRED says the startup's system targets gas turbines used for data-center power.
  • Supercritical CO2 could improve efficiency.
  • The approach would not make fossil-powered generation emissions-free.
