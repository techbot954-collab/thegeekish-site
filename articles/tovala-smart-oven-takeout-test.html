Tovala's smart oven pitch is convenience, not chef magic

MC
By Mika Circuitgadgets, creator gear, and PCs reporter

Based on TechCrunch's two-week Tovala smart oven test and Tovala product details.

The connected oven scans meal QR codes, changes cooking modes automatically, and tries to beat the "too tired to cook" dinner problem.

SMART KITCHENGeekish sourced quick read

Original Geekish context based on the sources linked below.

The short version

TechCrunch tested Tovala's smart oven for two weeks and found the value proposition is convenience. Tovala's meals arrive mostly prepped, users scan a QR code in the app, and the oven automatically follows the cooking instructions.

What the oven does

The countertop oven can bake, broil, air fry, toast, and reheat. TechCrunch reports the regular oven costs $299, while the $349 Smart Oven Pro adds steam. Tovala's meal service offers more than 45 meals each week and plans from 4 to 16 meals.

The tradeoff

The food is not positioned as a replacement for a great home-cooked meal. TechCrunch liked the ease and some dishes, including a pineapple shrimp stir-fry, but called out meat texture as a weak spot and noted pricing starts at $13.99 for single-serving meals, plus shipping.

Geekish take

The clever part is not just meal kits. Tovala also supports compatible grocery products through barcode scanning, which gives the oven a reason to stay useful even if someone pauses the subscription.

Quick takeaways

  • Tovala pairs a connected oven with QR-code meal kits.
  • The strongest pitch is low-effort dinner, not restaurant-level cooking.
  • Barcode support for grocery items makes it more flexible than a single-purpose meal-kit machine.
