Toyota workers are training humanoid robots for factories

MC
By Mika Circuitgadgets, creator gear, and PCs reporter

Based on Ars Technica and Nikkei Asia reporting.

The next factory robot story is not only automation. It is humans teaching machines how factory work actually feels.

FACTORY ROBOTSGeekish sourced quick read

Original Geekish context based on the sources linked below.

The short version

Ars Technica reports Toyota workers are already helping train humanoid robots as the automaker works toward a major factory robotics expansion. Ars, citing Nikkei Asia, says Toyota aims to begin investing $6.42 billion per year in factory upgrades starting in 2028.

The training part

According to the report, Toyota workers are teaching ELEY humanoid robots precise hand movements using jigs based on the robots' fingers. That detail matters because factory work is full of small movements that are obvious to people and difficult for machines.

Why it matters

Toyota's reported target includes 150,000 robots in its own automotive plants and another 250,000 at group company facilities. A Toyota executive told Nikkei Asia the push is not meant to directly replace humans, but the scale makes the labor question impossible to ignore.

Geekish take

The interesting bit is the loop: human workers train robots, robots reshape the floor, and then humans have to work inside that new system. The robotics future is not just metal bodies; it is workplace design.

Quick takeaways

  • Ars reports Toyota workers are helping train humanoid factory robots.
  • Nikkei Asia reported Toyota's planned robot push could start in 2028.
  • Toyota says the effort is not meant to directly replace humans.
