Truecaller is taking scam checks beyond caller ID

The company is turning its scam intelligence into a web tool for suspicious numbers, links, and messages, starting in India.

MC
Updated September 28, 2026

Concise Geekish brief based on TechCrunch and Truecaller's Scam Checker launch.

What happened

Truecaller launched Scam Checker, a free web and Android service that lets people paste a suspicious phone number, link, or message to check whether it looks fraudulent. TechCrunch reports that the tool works without requiring the Truecaller app or a sign-in.

The initial rollout is in India, with expansion planned for Latin America, the Middle East and Africa, and Southeast Asia. More detailed caller-ID information remains inside Truecaller's existing signed-in service.

Why it matters

Scams are no longer just phone calls. Truecaller told TechCrunch that Scam Checker expands shortened URLs, follows redirects, compares results with its risk database, and surfaces community reports from ScamFeed.

The launch also shows Truecaller adapting as its caller-ID business faces pressure from telecom, Apple, and Google spam-protection features. The pitch is simple: if scam attempts moved into messages and links, protection has to follow them there.

Quick takeaways

  • Truecaller says it serves more than 500 million monthly users.
  • TechCrunch reports ScamFeed has about 20,000 live scam reports in India and about 1,300 new reports weekly.
  • Truecaller says screenshot uploads and broader scam detection are planned future additions.
