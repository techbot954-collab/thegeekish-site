Una Watch makes the $199 sports watch feel hackable again

MC
By Mika Circuitgadgets, creator gear, and practical weirdness

Based on The Verge reporting and Una's product/open-source pages.

Most sports watches are sealed little fitness appliances. Una is trying the opposite pitch: cheaper, modular, repairable, and open enough for people to build on.

Original Geekish context based on the sources linked below.

The short version

The Verge reports that the Una Watch is now priced at $199, down from its earlier $350 Kickstarter offer. It is a GPS sports watch built around a modular, repairable design.

Why it stands out

The watch has a 1.2-inch MIP LCD display and up to 10 days of battery life, according to The Verge. Una also says the project is open-source, including its SDK, housing, and circuit boards.

Geekish take

The exciting part is not that one watch can beat Garmin or Apple overnight. It is that a modern fitness wearable is being framed as something owners can repair, inspect, and extend. That is a very different vibe from the usual "buy the new one when the old one ages out" cycle.

Quick takeaways

  • Una Watch is listed at $199 after an earlier $350 Kickstarter price.
  • The Verge highlights its modular, repairable hardware pitch.
  • Una says its SDK, housing, and circuit boards are open-source.
