YouTube custom feeds give viewers a knob for the algorithm

JP
By Jax PixelApps, social tech, and internet culture

Based on WIRED and Engadget reporting from YouTube's latest update.

YouTube's creator-side AI push got the spotlight, but the viewer side matters too: custom feeds are about letting people steer the recommendation machine without fully escaping it.

STREAMINGGeekish sourced quick read

Original Geekish context based on the sources linked below.

The short version

WIRED reports that YouTube's custom feeds will let viewers tweak what the algorithm shows on their front page and curate recommendations around a specific vibe. Engadget also reports that YouTube will soon support GIFs in comments.

Why it matters

This is not the same as a chronological subscription feed. It is still recommendation-driven, but with more user direction layered on top. For people whose homepages keep drifting, a custom feed could become a cleaner way to separate music, learning, microdramas, gaming, and comfort-watch loops.

Geekish take

The healthier version of recommendations probably is not "no algorithm." It is more visible controls, less mystery, and fewer weird resets where one accidental click takes over your homepage for a week.

Quick takeaways

  • WIRED says custom feeds will let viewers steer front-page recommendations.
  • Engadget reports YouTube will also bring GIFs to comments.
  • The change is viewer-facing, while YouTube also expands creator-side AI tools.
